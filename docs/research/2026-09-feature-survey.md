# Feature survey, September 2026

What recognized PDF libraries and the PDF standards offer that this project's roadmap did not plan, as found on
2026-09-26, and what the maintainer decided about each. It is the source of the roadmap revision of that day,
of ADRs 37 to 44, and of the comparison published with the documentation.

> A snapshot, not a reference. Claims about third-party products and standards are what their public
> documentation said when the survey read it; each was checked once by an independent pass, not
> continuously. Verify one before relying on it.

## Method

Six researchers each covered one area, reading current public sources and, for every feature, searching
this repository's documents to decide whether a milestone, a decision or a debt row already planned it:

| Area | What was read |
|---|---|
| .NET libraries | iText 9 and its add-ons, Aspose.PDF, Syncfusion, PDFsharp and MigraDoc, QuestPDF, Docotic.Pdf, GemBox.Pdf, Nutrient, Apryse, IronPDF, Spire.PDF, PdfPig, Telerik, DevExpress |
| Other ecosystems | PDFBox, OpenPDF, qpdf, MuPDF and PyMuPDF, pdfcpu, HexaPDF, pikepdf, pypdf, pdf-lib, pdf.js, PDFium, Poppler, Ghostscript, callas pdfToolbox, Acrobat Preflight, veraPDF |
| ISO 32000-2 | The PDF 2.0 specification clause by clause, with its errata |
| Standards around PDF | ISO/TS 32001 to 32005, PDF/A-1 to 4, PDF/UA-1 and 2, WTPDF, PDF Declarations, PDF/X, PDF/VT, PDF/E, PDF/R, XMP, the Arlington model, PAdES, Factur-X and ZUGFeRD, EN 16931, the French e-invoicing reform, payment QR codes |
| HTML to PDF engines | Prince, WeasyPrint, PDFreactor, Antenna House, Vivliostyle, Paged.js, iText pdfHTML, Chromium; CSS Paged Media, GCPM, Fragmentation, Text, Fonts, Color and the rest |
| Business workflows | Legal case files and French practice, redaction, Bates numbering, comparison, OCR, scans, sanitization, e-signature, batch generation, extraction for AI |

A second agent per area then tried to refute each finding — on the roadmap (was it really absent?), on the facts,
and on its relevance to business documents — and dropped 3. The 307 that survived were
merged across areas and grouped into sixteen themes. A last pass, meant to look for what the six had missed,
did not run: the session's usage limit stopped it. The maintainer then answered one question per decision,
recorded below.

## Inconsistencies the survey found in the roadmap

Each was corrected by the revision, by an ADR, or by a debt row.

- M4 (docs/milestones/M4.md, and roadmap.md in M4's text) assigns 'conformance remediation — embedding missing fonts, adding an output intent or XMP' to M12. M12's deliverables and acceptance list only generation, Factur-X, preservation on merge and profiles, so no milestone converts a received PDF to PDF/A.
- CLAUDE.md D17 says 'PAdES later', ADR 18 says 'PAdES follows', architecture.md lists Signing as 'PAdES (late satellite)' and the site says 'PAdES signing, Planned, M14'. M14's deliverables name only IPdfSigner, a local implementation and an HSM path: no baseline level, no TSA, DSS or LTV, no certification or signature field. No milestone verifies a signature, although M3 and M14 both assert that existing signatures survive.
- ADR 5 says SkiaSharp is allowed 'in .Html only', yet architecture.md §2 gives AdCodicem.Pdf.Rendering a SkiaSharp dependency and ADR 16 / M14 plan 'Skia rasterization'. ADR 5 needs amending before M14, and before any imaging satellite that uses Skia codecs.
- ADR 5 lists 'bidirectional text' among what HarfBuzz provides. HarfBuzz does not implement UAX #9 (run resolution and reordering are the caller's job), so bidi is in fact unplanned.
- M1 only passes DCT, JPX, CCITT and JBIG2 through. M10 promises 'extraction of images' (output format unstated), M13 'recompression' and M14 rasterization of the reference and scan documents, but Skia decodes neither JBIG2, CCITT nor (in SkiaSharp) JPEG 2000, and no milestone plans the decoders. M14's acceptance on scans depends on work nobody owns.
- M12's title says 'PDF/A-3' but its deliverables say 'PDF/A-2b and PDF/A-3b', and 'PDF/A and PDF/UA profiles' name no parts. M12's acceptance ('the profiles agree with veraPDF on every corpus document') implicitly requires PDF/A-1a/1b, PDF/A-4f and PDF/UA-1 profiles, because corpus files claim them.
- M8 requires passing 'PDF/UA validation' without naming the part. PDF/UA-2 requires PDF 2.0 output, but M3 specifies no output-version policy, and Factur-X (M12) still requires PDF/A-3 (PDF 1.7-based). The M8 choice constrains M3, and no document records it.
- M8's acceptance names 'the two-column report' among the generated reference documents, yet M7 has no multi-column layout deliverable, and ADR 4's list (box model, tables, paged media, flexbox, simple grid, inline SVG) does not include it.
- M11 plans the password (standard) security handler only, and its acceptance says every encrypted corpus document opens 'with its recorded password'. Invariant 12 says every PDF valid under ISO 32000 is readable, and the public-key handler (§7.6.5) is part of ISO 32000-2, so M11 should at least detect such files and report them under a stable code.
- M5's merge list (bookmarks, links, annotations, attachments) and ADR 17's recombination list (structure trees, OutputIntents, metadata, fonts) both omit /PageLabels, /OCProperties, /AcroForm (with field-name collisions), the Names trees (Dests, EmbeddedFiles, JavaScript), /Extensions, /ViewerPreferences and /OpenAction. Each is silently dropped or misapplied on merge, against invariant 7, and M5's acceptance tests none of them.
- CLAUDE.md makes 'table of contents, bookmarks, continuous pagination' the first business priority, but M5 depends only on M3: numbering stamps are M9 (which needs M6) and a TOC page needs M6/M7 text layout. The priority cannot be met at M5, and only /PageLabels, which are unplanned, could give continuous pagination there.
- M9's acceptance ('every untouched object byte-identical', PDF/A loss reported) would pass while silently voiding a DocMDP P=1 certification or breaking the tag tree of a PDF/UA input. Invariant 7 covers tagged structure, but M9 tests PDF/A only, and signatures not at all.
- M11 fills AcroForms, but the corpus notes list hybrid and dynamic XFA, Reader-extended (UR3) forms and JavaScript-calculated fields for M11. The milestone says nothing about stale XFA data after filling, UR3 breakage or stale calculated totals, all of which are silent losses.
- architecture.md §3 describes Content/ as 'the content stream interpreter used by extraction (M8)', but extraction is M10 (M8 is tagged structure).
- CLAUDE.md 'Known traps' lists Resources, MediaBox and Rotate as inherited page attributes but omits CropBox, which ISO 32000-2 Table 31 also makes inheritable and which stamping (M9), extraction and rasterization must use.
- M7.2 'page counters' (page X of Y), M7.6 'a table of contents with real page numbers' and group totals are forward references. The forward-only writer (ADR 13) and M7's 'memory constant at 1000 pages' acceptance need a stated strategy (a two-pass anchor-to-page map, or late-filled Form XObjects), and none is recorded.
- Debt T06 covers only PDFDocEncoding in PdfString.ToText, but the same shipped method also decodes PDF 2.0 UTF-8 text strings (BOM EF BB BF) as Latin-1, which garbles accented bookmarks, labels and Info values from PDF 2.0 producers.
- ADR 36 defers a public rule API to M12 ('the first consumer that needs them public'), yet M12's deliverables do not mention it. ADR 36's severity scale (Information, Warning, Error) also has no outcome for PDF/UA conditions that need human judgment.
- ADR 11 makes remote fetching opt-in for @font-face only. No ADR or milestone governs how the HTML engine loads images, stylesheets, SVG or attachments (http, file:, redirects, private addresses), and invariant 4's 'everything read is hostile' is stated for PDF input only.
- ADR 4 names 'labels' in the intended use, but no milestone plans barcodes. M7.4's 'simple grid' is never defined, so users cannot know what degrades.
- architecture.md §2 lists AdCodicem.Pdf.Signing as depending on AdCodicem.Pdf only, but CMS (SignedCms, Rfc3161TimestampToken, EnvelopedCms) lives in the out-of-band System.Security.Cryptography.Pkcs package. It also promises FacturX 'validation' without a rule set, and the official EN 16931 Schematron is XSLT 2.0, which .NET cannot execute.
- M13's 'recompression' is undefined: lossless or lossy, whether images are decoded, which codecs. The small decision that copied streams travel encoded concerns copying only. For legal exhibits, the lossy policy (opt-in, reported, lossless-only JBIG2) needs to be written down.
- M10's acceptance only requires a scan to report 'no extractable text'. No milestone ever adds text to scans, although scans are the majority of third-party material in case files.

## Themes

Coverage is the roadmap's as it stood before the revision: *absent* — nothing planned it; *partial* — a
milestone touched it, not the part described. Relevance is to invoices, reports, contracts and case files.

### Case-file assembly completeness

Case-file assembly is the first business priority, and M5 lists only insert, remove, reorder, rotate, extract and a merge that keeps bookmarks, links, annotations and attachments. Several catalog-level structures are dropped or misapplied on merge today: page labels, name trees, AcroForm, optional content, /Extensions, viewer preferences. Invariant 7 forbids that silent loss. The 'continuous pagination' named in CLAUDE.md can only be delivered at M5 through /PageLabels, because numbering stamps need M6 fonts.

**Decided**: M6 (merge fidelity, page labels, attachments, viewer preferences) and M7 (split, image pages, pruning, outlines, links between pieces, portfolios).

### Legal exhibit workflow

A whole niche of French SaaS (Kleos, Hub-Avocat, L'Autotamponneur) sells exactly this, so a .NET library that ships these primitives, with a case-file model driving stamp, bookmark and index together, turns the first business priority into a product.

**Decided**: M9 (exhibit stamps, Bates numbering) and M18, the `AdCodicem.Pdf.CaseFile` satellite (piece model, inventory, portal presets, integrity manifest, e-mail to PDF).

### Redaction and sanitization

It also extends invariant 4 from how a file parses to what it does: JavaScript, Launch actions and hidden text are also a prompt-injection channel for LLM ingestion.

### Signatures: validation, revisions, PAdES levels

Contracts and case files arrive signed (eIDAS seals, DocuSign, notarial deeds), and whether an exhibit is authentic and unmodified is a core legal question. Yet no milestone verifies a signature, and M14 names no PAdES level, TSA, DSS or certification. iText's 2025-26 releases center on validation, and PDFsharp 6.2 now signs for free. The core already walks the /Prev chain, so revision history and byte-range coverage are cheap and need no cryptography.

**Decided**: M4 (revisions, byte-range coverage, change classification, in the core, no cryptography), M26 (PAdES B-B and B-T), M27 (B-LT, B-LTA, validation); ADR 41.

### Security handlers and forms

Invariant 12 also asks that a valid public-key-encrypted file at least be recognized. Field creation (programmatic and from HTML) and data exchange are the business-facing additions.

### E-invoicing, payment codes and barcodes

### .NET libraries

Method: I read CLAUDE.md, docs/roadmap.md, docs/architecture.md, the ADR index and the scope ADRs (3, 4, 8, 11, 15, 16, 18), M2.md, M4.md and the status.md debt table. Market trends (2025-2026): 1. Signatures have moved from signing to validation. iText 9.0 to 9.6 added a validation API, EU trusted lists (LOTL), qualified-status checks and non-EU lists. Aspose added TryVerifySignature, compromise detection and UnsignedContentAbsorber. Docotic, GemBox and Syncfusion all advertise LTV and B-LTA. 2. AI and RAG extraction: Aspose PDF-to-Markdown plus chunking and embedding (26.5); Syncfusion markets a deterministic Smart Data Extractor with no AI dependency (2026 Vol 1); Apryse and Nutrient sell AI key-value extraction. A deterministic, managed, layout-aware export is a defensible niche for this library. 5. Security hygiene is packaged: redaction everywhere, and sanitization (Apryse; Aspose HiddenDataSanitizer in 25.11). 6. Strongest case-file gaps (all absent or partial): page labels, Bates numbering, page resize and normalization, split strategies, OCR, image and TIFF to PDF, e-mail to PDF, conversion of third-party PDFs to PDF/A, font subset consolidation, image optimization, redaction and sanitization, signature validation, and viewer preferences (open on the bookmark pane).

Architecture points the orchestrator may want to ask the maintainer about: - System.Security.Cryptography.Pkcs (CMS) is an out-of-band NuGet package, not part of the shared framework. Signature validation, PAdES and public-key encryption therefore either stay in the Signing satellite or need a minimal CMS implemented over System.Formats.Asn1, which is in the shared framework. - Image codecs (JBIG2, JPX, JPEG encoding, resampling) bear on invariant 1 and ADR 5. ADR 5 says Skia is allowed in .Html only, while architecture.md §2 also gives Rendering a SkiaSharp dependency. Using Skia for optimization or OCR preprocessing would need that clarified. - OCR engines (Tesseract native, ONNX Runtime) conflict with AOT, trimming and determinism, so they belong in a satellite behind an engine abstraction. - Office-to-PDF would reopen ADR 2 and ADR 4. - Online OCSP, CRL, LOTL and TSA access conflicts with determinism (invariant 6). It should follow the ADR 11 model: off by default, with inputs the caller can inject.

Could not verify: - Spire.PDF's What's-New page returned HTTP 403, so Spire's 2025-26 feature list comes from its README and tutorials only. - Apryse's feature-index page returned 404; its product page, sanitization and redaction docs were used instead. - The exact current CSS support list of pdfHTML: running elements and footnotes are asserted from secondary sources; target-counter and page margin boxes are confirmed. - Whether GemBox.Pdf generates PDF/A. - Whether Telerik has redaction. - Whether QuestPDF hyphenates. - iText's support for ISO/TS 32001 and 32002: only 32003 and 32004 are confirmed for 9.0. SHA-3 is confirmed for Aspose 25.1.

### ISO 32000-2

What the specification is today: there is no new ISO edition of 32000-2 since December 2020. The '2024 revision' is the PDF Association's no-cost sponsored edition, carrying Errata Collection 2 (errata to 2024-07-20; 265 corrections, 603 edits). That was replaced on 2026-06-18 by Errata Collection 3 (356 corrections, 766 edits, 86 % ISO-approved), which also absorbs the ISO/TS 32005 namespace-mapping corrections that affect PDF/UA-2. Around the core sits a family of extensions: ISO/TS 32001 (SHA-3), 32002 (ECDSA/EdDSA), 32003 (AES-GCM), 32004 (MAC integrity for encrypted files), 32005 (1.7/2.0 structure-namespace inclusion) and 32007 (glTF in RichMedia).

Sources: https://pdfa.org/pdf-2-0-errata-collection-3-now-available/, https://pdfa.org/pdf-2-0-errata-collection-2-is-now-available/, https://pdf-issues.pdfa.org/32000-2-2020/.

Method: - I walked clause 7 to clause 14 and Annexes A-Q from the heading index of the 2020 sponsored text (Internet Archive, https://archive.org/details/iso-32000-2-2020-sponsored). - Clause and table numbers above come from that text; the EC2/EC3 renumbering, if any, was not checked edit by edit. - pdfa.org and the ANSI preview returned 403, so errata-collection facts come from search snippets and a third-party summary (zenn.dev). - Library claims were checked against current documentation where cited (iText 9 security and validation, qpdf 12 CLI, pyHanko, PyMuPDF scrub/bake/redactions, pdfcpu, pypdf, PDFBox, Prince, WeasyPrint forms, PDFsharp 6.2 signatures, BFO and iText PDF/UA-2). A few are from knowledge and should be spot-checked: iText PdfAnnotationFlattener, qpdf's UTF-8 handling, PDFBox's name-tree merge.

Three things found in the repository rather than the specification: 1. src/AdCodicem.Pdf/Objects/PdfString.cs ToText() decodes PDF 2.0 UTF-8 strings (EF BB BF) as Latin-1. Debt T06 covers only PDFDocEncoding. 2. CLAUDE.md 'Known traps' lists Resources, MediaBox and Rotate as inherited but not CropBox, which ISO 32000-2 Table 31 also makes inheritable. 3. The CMS APIs (SignedCms, EnvelopedCms) are in the System.Security.Cryptography.Pkcs NuGet package, not the shared framework (https://www.nuget.org/packages/System.Security.Cryptography.Pkcs/). Signature validation, public-key encryption and the ISO/TS 32004 MAC therefore cannot live in the dependency-free core unless CMS is parsed over the inbox System.Formats.Asn1.

Where the market is moving: - Security: iText 9 shipped ISO/TS 32003/32004 and a signature-validation module. - Size reduction: qpdf 12.1 added --remove-structure and --jpeg-quality for smaller files. - Accessibility: PDF/UA-2 and WTPDF (2024) plus the European Accessibility Act (in force since 2025-06-28) are pushing libraries to PDF 2.0 tagging. BFO, iText, PDFlib and veraPDF already support it. - Manipulation: sanitize and redact are the headline features of PyMuPDF (scrub, apply_redactions) and iText (pdfSweep). - Signatures: pyHanko's revision diff analysis is the reference design for 'what changed since signing'.

Highest-value gaps for this roadmap: - redaction - page labels - signature validation with revision history - OCR text layer and scan compression - sanitization - tag-preserving stamping and the PDF/UA-2 choice - name-tree collisions on merge - general embedded-file and XMP APIs

Questions for the maintainer: - Default output version (PDF 1.7 or 2.0), and whether to target PDF/UA-1 or PDF/UA-2 before M8 starts. - Is true redaction in scope, and in which milestone after M10? - Is signature validation in scope, and does CMS live in the Signing satellite or a new one? - Do image codecs (JBIG2, JPX, CCITT, DCT) go in a new managed satellite, and do we accept a managed JPEG encoder outside .Html (D04)? - Should OCR be supported as 'accept external OCR results' only? - Should sanitization be a first-class feature? - Should the prepress, multimedia/3D, web-capture, navigator and XFA-rendering items be excluded explicitly by ADR, so they stop coming back?

### HTML to PDF engines

Method: I read CLAUDE.md, docs/roadmap.md (M7 is only the six-line sub-milestone list; there is no docs/milestones/M7.md), ADRs 2-7, 11 and 18, architecture.md, the status.md debt table, corpus.md and corpus-contributions.md. Almost nothing in the CSS/typography/graphics space is written down beyond the M7.1-M7.6 headings, so most items are 'partial' (named by an M7 heading with no mechanism) or 'absent'.

Inconsistencies found in the repo docs: (1) ADR 5 credits HarfBuzz with 'bidirectional text'. HarfBuzz does not implement UAX #9 (its manual says the buffer must already be in visual order), so bidi is unplanned work. (2) M8's acceptance names 'the two-column report' among the generated reference documents, but M7 has no multi-column deliverable.
