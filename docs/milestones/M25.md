# M25 — Rasterisation

> State: to do — Depends on: M15, M22 — Skia placed by [ADR 43](../adr/0043-skia-in-html-and-rendering-and-text-analysis-is-ours.md); a satellite after the foundations by [ADR 16](../adr/0016-rasterisation-as-a-satellite-after-the-foundations.md); every pixel of a scan decoded by the codecs of [ADR 42](../adr/0042-image-codecs-and-scans.md)

## Goal

Render any page the reader opens to pixels — previews, thumbnails, M24's visual comparison and the visual tests
of every later milestone — through the interpreter extraction reads with and the decoders M22 wrote, drawing what
a viewer shows for a given intent, hidden layers and annotation flags included, with memory that follows the page
and the raster rather than the file.

Since M9 every milestone that changed what a page looks like has asked MuPDF or pdf.js, in a container, whether
it still looks right, because the library could not look at a page itself. A case file needs more than a verdict
in CI: a thumbnail of each piece for the person who checks the bordereau, a preview for a portal upload, the
visual diff of a contract returned for signature, and — M4's open case — the one change only a rendering sees, a
field appearance laid over signed text. The failures this milestone exists to prevent are the ordinary ones: a
JBIG2 scan drawn as a black page because nothing decoded it; a watermark layer that is off by default drawn
anyway, or an OCR layer drawn in black over the scan it transcribes; a 35,000-pixel CCITT page that decodes to
153 MB taking five gigabytes once expanded to RGBA; a Type 3 font that reaches itself hanging the thread; an
embedded font program handed to a native font parser — the attack surface ADR 42 keeps away from image codecs
— or to the system's font manager, so that Windows and Linux render the same page differently; a page rendered
without the `/Rotate` or the `/CropBox` it inherits.

## Scope

**In**:

- the **`AdCodicem.Pdf.Rendering` satellite**, depending on the core, `AdCodicem.Pdf.Imaging` and SkiaSharp —
  its CPU raster backend only, and on Linux its `NoDependencies` native assets, since Skia's font manager is never
  used (below);
- the **device seam made public**: M15's `IPdfContentDevice`, internal until its first outside consumer, becomes
  public in the core; and the core's **page-raster seam**, `IPdfPageRasteriser` and `PdfRaster` — which M22 put
  beside it for OCR and M24's visual comparison consumes —, implemented here, so that the satellite depends on
  neither `AdCodicem.Pdf.Compare` nor the OCR orchestration, and M22's `PdfOcr` rasterises through it the pages that
  are not one image (below);
- a **Skia device** that paints everything the interpreter reports: paths filled and stroked with the whole line
  state, clipping by paths and by text, the eight text rendering modes, glyphs from **outlines the core reads**
  (TrueType, CFF, Type 1 and CID-keyed programs, Type 3 procedures through the interpreter), images and stencil
  masks decoded by M22 and **downsampled while they are decoded**, every colour space through M22's evaluation,
  the seven shading types, tiling patterns, transparency groups, soft masks, the sixteen blend modes;
- **intents**: `View` (the default) and `Print`, through M11's `PdfLayerVisibility` for optional content and the
  annotation flags for annotations; annotation and widget **appearances** placed as M15 places them, the
  appearance state honoured, missing appearances generated in memory through M11's and M16's generators on
  request, never written;
- **page geometry**: the box rendered (`CropBox` by default, through inherited resolution, clipped to the
  `MediaBox`), `/Rotate` inherited, `UserUnit`, a resolution in dots per inch or a target size, a paper or
  transparent background;
- **band rendering** for rasters too large to hold, rows streamed to a sink; memory bounded by the band and by the
  images at device scale, whatever the page;
- **outputs**: the raw raster (`Gray8`, `Rgb24`, `Rgba32`), PNG through the core's encoder, JPEG and WebP through
  Skia's encoders on request; **thumbnails**, the page's embedded `/Thumb` first when asked;
- **M24's visual comparison run in-process** on these rasters, its visual row closed here on our own rasteriser;
- the command-line tool's **`render`** verb, and `compare --visual` without a raster supplied by the caller;
- a report per render: fonts substituted, images decoded and at what scale, colours approximated, features
  approximated, each under a `render.*` code.

**Out, explicitly**:

- **colour-managed conversion** — ICC transforms for `ICCBased` spaces beyond their alternate, output intents
  simulated, overprint simulated, separation previews — **M29**, whose managed colour-management satellite plugs
  into the converter seam defined here; until then `ICCBased`, `Lab` and CMYK content are converted by M22's
  evaluation and reported (`render.colour-approximated`);
- **transparency flattening** and **rasterising a page into the document** (a "print as image" redaction, or
  PDF/A-1 conversion) — not planned (M19 and M20 say so); M25 renders, nothing writes the raster back;
- **vector outputs** (SVG, XPS, EMF) and **GPU** backends — not planned;
- **printing** — spooling to a printer — not planned; the `Print` intent renders what a printer would receive;
- **executing JavaScript** before drawing, and **rendering dynamic XFA** — never
  ([ADR 37](../adr/0037-out-of-scope-active-content-and-pdf-to-office.md)): a dynamic XFA form renders its
  placeholder page, as its PDF says; 3D, screen and rich-media annotations render their appearance, the poster;
- **text shaping** of any kind: a page's glyphs are already chosen; generating a missing appearance for Arabic or
  Hebrew text is M11's and M16's simple path, reported by them (`text.shaping-required`);
- the **core in browser WebAssembly** is M23's; SkiaSharp's WebAssembly assets are not tested here.

## Design

### Where it lives

| Part | Where | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `IPdfContentDevice`, the interpreter's state it receives, the glyph outline sink | Core, `Content/` and `Fonts/`, made public | M15 wrote them; M19 extends them; a device outside the core needs them public, and nothing about them needs Skia |
| `IPdfPageRasteriser`, `PdfRaster` | Core, `Content/`, where M22 put them | Compare and the OCR orchestration depend on the core alone, and Rendering must depend on neither; all three meet at the core's seam |
| `IPdfColourConverter` | Core, `Graphics/`, beside M22's colour evaluation | M29's colour-management satellite implements it without SkiaSharp; declared in `.Rendering`, it would drag Skia into that satellite |
| The Skia device, the rasteriser, bands, thumbnails, the encoders | `AdCodicem.Pdf.Rendering` | SkiaSharp is allowed here and never in the core (ADR 43) |
| JBIG2, JPEG 2000 and JPEG decoding | `AdCodicem.Pdf.Imaging` (M22) | ADR 42; CCITT stays in the core |
| Colour spaces and functions evaluated | M22's evaluation, where M22 put it | One evaluator for image export, redaction of pixels and rendering |
| The OFL faces used for substitution | `AdCodicem.Pdf.Fonts`, where M8 puts the set (decided 2026-09-27), Liberation Sans regular in the core | ADR 11: never the system's fonts |

The satellite takes SkiaSharp's native library and no other native code. It never asks Skia for a typeface, a font
manager or an image codec: glyph outlines come from the core, pixels from M22. Skia draws paths, images and
layers into a raster — the part of it that is small, fuzzed by its own authors and deterministic per version.

### The rasteriser

```
PdfRasteriser            thread-safe engine (invariant 8); implements IPdfPageRasteriser
  Render(page, PdfRasterOptions, cancellationToken)              -> PdfRaster (pooled, disposable)
  RenderRows(page, options, IPdfRasterRowSink, progress, token)  -> PdfRasterReport, rows streamed in order
  RenderThumbnail(page, maxWidth, maxHeight, options, token)     -> PdfRaster
PdfRasterOptions         immutable: resolution (dpi, or fit into a box), box (Crop by default, Media, Trim,
                         Bleed, Art), intent (View, Print), layer configuration (M11's, /D by default), annotations
                         (All, None, a filter; widgets; popups never), missing appearances (Skip, GenerateInMemory),
                         background (paper white, transparent), pixel format, anti-aliasing (text, paths, images),
                         image smoothing (honour /Interpolate, always, never), limits (below)
PdfRaster                (core) width, height, stride, format, a pooled buffer, the page frame it covers
IPdfRasterRowSink        receives rows in order, a band at a time; the PNG writer is one
PdfRasterEncoder         Png(raster or rows) through the core's encoder; Jpeg(raster, quality) and
                         WebP(raster, quality) through Skia's, lossy and on request only
PdfRasterReport          per page: the render.* diagnostics, fonts substituted, images decoded and the scale they
                         were decoded at, colours approximated, features approximated, bands, peak pooled bytes
```

A render opens nothing: the caller opens the document, as for every operation, with its `PdfReaderOptions`; the
engine holds no per-document state between calls but a bounded glyph cache keyed by the document's identity, which
the caller may disable. Two threads may render two documents at once; one document is rendered by one thread at a
time, as `PdfDocument` is not thread-safe. M3's cancellation and progress convention applies, stage `Rendering`,
units the bands.

### The device

The interpreter (M15) runs the page, its forms, patterns, Type 3 glyphs and annotation appearances; the device
receives what they paint, already in user space with the CTM, and draws it into an `SKCanvas` over a raster
`SKSurface` for the current band.

- **Paths**: every segment of `m l c v y h re`; fill with the non-zero or even-odd rule; stroke with width, cap,
  join, miter limit and dash array and phase, in user space transformed by the CTM, so that a non-uniform CTM
  gives the elliptical pen the specification's stroking rules ask for; a zero width drawn as the thinnest line the device can
  render, one pixel, as §8.4.3.2 requires; stroke adjustment (`SA`) honoured by snapping axis-aligned strokes to
  the pixel grid; a dash array of zeros drawn solid, as viewers do, and reported.
- **Clipping**: `W` and `W*` intersect the clip at the next painting operator; clip by text (modes 4 to 7)
  accumulates the glyphs' outlines until `ET`; the clip is part of the state M15 saves and restores, and the device
  mirrors it with `Save` and `Restore` on the canvas, never by recomputing it.
- **Text**: each glyph's outline, transformed by the text rendering matrix, filled, stroked or both by its mode;
  mode 3 draws nothing; mode 7 only clips. Horizontal scaling, rise, character and word spacing are the
  interpreter's; the device receives positioned glyphs.
- **Images and stencil masks** (below); **inline images** as images, through M8's reader.
- **Shadings**: types 2 and 3 (axial, radial) as Skia gradients when their function sampled into stops equals the
  function at every stop within half a colour step — otherwise, and for type 1 (function-based), evaluated per
  device pixel in the band; types 4 to 7 (free-form and lattice triangle meshes, Coons and tensor patches)
  subdivided into triangles no larger than a device pixel's flatness, drawn with Skia's per-vertex colours; `sh`
  and shading patterns both; `/Extend`, `/Background` (for patterns only, as the specification says) and `/BBox`.
- **Tiling patterns**: coloured and uncoloured; one cell rendered into an image at device resolution, then tiled
  by an image shader with the pattern matrix — never cell by cell, so that a cell a point wide over a page costs
  one cell's rendering; a cell smaller than a device pixel is filled with its average colour and reported.
- **Transparency**: constant alpha (`CA`, `ca`), the sixteen blend modes of §11.3.5 mapped to Skia's
  `SKBlendMode` of the same definition — an unknown mode is `Normal`, as the specification says, and reported —
  **isolated groups** as layers, **non-isolated groups** drawn straight onto the backdrop when their opacity and
  blend mode make that exact, and otherwise as layers initialised from the backdrop, reported where that departs
  from the specification's group compositing, **soft masks** by luminosity
  (with `/BC` and `/TR`) and by alpha, from `SMask` in the graphics state and from an image's `/SMask`; the page
  group's colour space for blending when it is gray or RGB, CMYK groups blended in RGB and reported.
  **Knockout groups** are drawn as non-knockout and reported (`render.knockout-approximated`): Skia has no
  knockout compositing, business documents rarely carry one, and an exact implementation costs a layer per object.
- **Transfer functions and halftones** — `TR`, `TR2`, `HT` — are device-dependent: `TR` is applied as a colour
  filter when it is an identity or a sampled function, halftones are ignored, as screen viewers do, and both are
  reported once per document.
- **Overprint** (`OP`, `op`, `OPM`) is ignored in RGB output, as viewers do by default; its simulation is M29's.

### Glyphs are ours

- **Outlines come from the core.** M8 reads `glyf` and `loca` and interprets Type 2 charstrings; M15 reads Type 1
  programs. This milestone adds, in the core's `Fonts/`, an outline sink those readers emit into: TrueType
  quadratic contours and composite glyphs (walked iteratively, as M8 already does, with their component matrices),
  CFF and CID-keyed CFF charstrings (`FDSelect` and `/FontMatrix` per font dictionary), Type 1 charstrings with
  their `seac` accents, flex and hint replacement read and hints ignored. M8's guard, `MaxCharstringOperations`,
  bounds the charstring work.
- **No embedded font program ever reaches a native parser**, and no system font is ever consulted: FreeType,
  DirectWrite and CoreText are reached through Skia's font manager only, which is never created. Hostile fonts
  stay in the managed, bounded, fuzzed readers the core already has, and a page renders the same on every system.
- **No hinting.** Outlines are drawn unhinted and anti-aliased, as MuPDF and pdf.js draw them. A few TrueType
  faces — Chinese ones above all — assemble their glyphs with hinting instructions; a font whose glyphs collapse
  without them is detected by its name and its `fpgm` size, drawn as it is and reported
  (`render.font-needs-hinting`), since interpreting TrueType bytecode is a virtual machine of its own.
- **Non-embedded fonts** are substituted from the registry and the OFL set by the descriptor's flags, weight and
  name — sans, serif, monospace, italic, bold —, each glyph scaled horizontally to the width `/Widths` gives, as
  viewers do, and reported once per font (`render.font-substituted`). A symbolic font that cannot be substituted
  draws nothing and is reported; `.notdef` is drawn as the font draws it — the box PDF24 draws for every capital E
  is a box here too (`documents/invoice/pdf24-invoice-fr.pdf`): a rasteriser shows the page, it does not repair it.
- **A glyph cache** per document and font holds outlines in font units, bounded in bytes (an option) and evicted
  least recently used; transformed paths are never cached, since the text matrix varies.

### Images

- **Decoding** goes through M22: CCITT in the core, JBIG2 (with its globals), JPEG 2000 and JPEG in Imaging; Flate,
  LZW and the other filters through M1. An image that cannot be decoded is drawn as nothing — never as a black or
  grey box, which a reader would take for content — and reported (`render.image-undecodable`) with the object.
- **Decoded at the scale it is drawn at.** The image's device footprint — its unit square through the CTM and the
  device matrix — gives the number of device pixels it covers. When the samples outnumber them by a factor of two or
  more, rows are box-filtered into the smaller size **while they are decoded**, and JPEG and JPEG 2000 are asked
  for the reduced resolution their formats offer (DCT scaling by 1/2 to 1/8, JPEG 2000 resolution levels) when M22's
  decoder provides it. The 35,000-pixel CCITT square decodes into a few thousand rows of a few thousand grey pixels,
  never into 1.2 gigapixels.
- **Masks**: `/ImageMask` stencils painted with the fill colour (an uncoloured pattern included); `/Decode`
  honoured, for masks and for samples; `/Mask` as an explicit stencil or as a colour-key range; `/SMask` and
  `/SMaskInData` for JPEG 2000; `/Matte` un-premultiplied as §11.6.5.2 says.
- **Smoothing**: `/Interpolate` true draws with bilinear filtering, false with nearest neighbour when enlarging and
  the box filter when reducing; an option overrides both.
- **Decoded images are cached** per document and object, at the scale decoded, bounded in bytes — so that a scan is
  decoded once for all the bands of its page, and a logo once for all the pages that draw it.

### Colour

Every colour space goes through M22's evaluation to 8-bit sRGB: `DeviceGray`, `DeviceRGB` and `DeviceCMYK` by the
formulas M22 records, `CalGray` and `CalRGB` through their white point and matrix, `Lab` through its white point to
sRGB, `ICCBased` by its `/Alternate` or, without one, by the device space of its `/N`, `Indexed` through its base,
`Separation` and `DeviceN` through their tint transforms (`All` and `None` as §8.6.6.4 defines them), functions of
types 0, 2, 3 and 4 by M22's evaluator, the type 4 calculator under its bounded stack and budget. Everything that is
approximated — an ICC profile not applied, a CMYK conversion without a profile — is reported once per document, not
per object. The conversion is a seam, `IPdfColourConverter`, declared in the core (the table above), which M29's
colour-management satellite replaces; the device calls it per colour set and per image row, never per pixel through an
interface.

### Intents, layers and annotations

- **Optional content** is decided by M11's evaluator for the intent's event (`View` or `Print`) and the
  configuration the options name, `/D` by default. The interpreter still runs hidden content, since its state
  operators change what follows (M11's trap), and its paints reach the device flagged hidden: the device drops them.
- **Annotations** are drawn after the page content, in `/Annots` order, each by its normal appearance — the
  `/AS` state's in a subdictionary —, placed by §12.5.5's algorithm as M15 places it. The flags decide: `Hidden`
  never; `NoView` not under `View`; under `Print`, only those with `Print`; `NoZoom` and `NoRotate` as M11 draws
  them; an annotation's `/OC` through the evaluator. Popups are viewer furniture and never drawn; widgets are
  annotations like the others.
- **Missing appearances**: nothing is drawn, and the annotation is listed (`render.appearance-missing`), unless
  the options ask `GenerateInMemory` — then M11's `EnsureAppearances` and M16's field appearances build one into a
  scratch change set that is discarded after the page, never written (`render.appearance-generated`). A form with
  `NeedAppearances` true gets its widgets' appearances generated under that option, as viewers regenerate them.
- **Signature appearances** are appearances: Acrobat's legacy layered ones (`n0` to `n4`) are nested forms and
  draw as their forms say.

### Geometry and bands

- **The page frame**: the chosen box, through inherited resolution and clipped to the `MediaBox`; an inverted box
  normalised; `/Rotate` inherited, normalised modulo 360 and applied clockwise — a value not a multiple of 90 is
  read as 0, as pdf.js reads it, and reported (`render.rotate-invalid`); `UserUnit` scaling user space. The raster
  is `ceil(width × dpi / 72 × UserUnit)` pixels wide, the device matrix mapping the box's lower-left corner to the
  raster's bottom-left pixel.
- **Bands**: when a raster's bytes exceed `PdfRasterOptions.BandBytes`, the page is rendered in horizontal bands,
  the interpreter run once per band with the band as the device clip; paths, images and glyph runs whose device
  bounds miss the band are culled before any drawing; decoded images come from the image cache, so a band costs an
  interpretation, not a decoding. Rows go to the sink band by band; the raster is never whole in memory.
  `Render` on a raster larger than `MaxRasterPixels` refuses (`render.raster-too-large`) and names `RenderRows`.
- **Thumbnails** fit the chosen box into a size, at the resolution that gives; `PreferEmbeddedThumbnail` returns
  the page's `/Thumb` decoded (it is an image like any other) when it is present and not smaller than asked.

### Determinism

Invariant 6 holds as far as Skia lets it, and the documentation says exactly how far:

- **the same raster bytes** for the same document, options and SkiaSharp version on the same processor family,
  across runs, threads and bands — rendering in bands gives the rows rendering whole gives;
- **within the threshold** across processor families: Skia's raster pipeline chooses SSE, AVX or NEON code paths at
  run time, and their rounding differs in the last bit of a blended channel. The report names the SkiaSharp version
  and the path chosen;
- PNG output through the core's encoder at a fixed compression level is deterministic per .NET version; JPEG and
  WebP are Skia's, lossy, and asked for explicitly.

### Bounds, classified (invariant 12, [ADR 34](../adr/0034-every-valid-pdf-is-readable-and-the-readers-guards-are.md))

The rasteriser reads documents only through the core, whose guards apply — `MaxContentOperations`,
`MaxGraphicsStateDepth`, `MaxPageGlyphs` (M15), `MaxCharstringOperations` (M8), `MaxDecodedStreamLength` and M22's
decoder bounds. What it adds is bounded by options, since the raster's size is the caller's choice as much as the
file's:

| Bound | Kind | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Pixels of a raster held whole | Option, `PdfRasterOptions.MaxRasterPixels` (proposed 2²⁸, 1 GB in `Rgba32`), `render.raster-too-large`; `RenderRows` has no such bound | A valid page may be 200 inches square, and `UserUnit` enlarges it further |
| Bytes of a band | Option, `BandBytes` (proposed 64 MB) | Memory follows the band, not the page |
| Bytes of transparency layers alive at once | Option, `MaxLayerBytes`; past it, groups are composited without isolation and reported (`render.layer-budget`) | Each nested group or soft mask holds a layer of the band's size; nesting is bounded by `MaxGraphicsStateDepth`, but its product with the band's size is not |
| Glyph outlines and decoded images cached | Options, `GlyphCacheBytes`, `ImageCacheBytes`; least recently used evicted | Caches are a speed-up; eviction costs time, never correctness |
| Samples decoded per image | M22's decoders under `MaxDecodedStreamLength`; decode-time reduction to the device footprint | The dimensions come from the file |
| Mesh subdivision | Internal constant: triangles are subdivided until they are no larger than one device pixel, at most 16 levels | Past a device pixel, subdivision changes no pixel; 2¹⁶ triangles per edge exceeds any raster the options allow |
| A tiling cell's size | None needed | One cell is rendered once and tiled by a shader, whatever the cell count |
| A form, pattern or Type 3 glyph that reaches itself | M15's cycle cut, reported | Invalid content |

### Diagnostics

| Code | Severity | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `render.font-substituted` | Information | A non-embedded font drawn with a substitute, named, widths forced to `/Widths` |
| `render.font-needs-hinting` | Warning | A TrueType face whose glyphs are assembled by instructions, drawn unhinted |
| `render.glyph-undrawable` | Warning | A glyph whose outline cannot be read, drawn empty |
| `render.image-undecodable` | Warning | An image no decoder could decode, drawn as nothing |
| `render.image-reduced` | Information | An image decoded at a reduced scale, and the factor |
| `render.colour-approximated` | Information | ICC, Lab or CMYK content converted without colour management |
| `render.knockout-approximated`, `render.transfer-ignored`, `render.halftone-ignored` | Information | A feature drawn approximately or ignored, as screen viewers do |
| `render.blend-mode-unknown` | Warning | An unknown blend mode, drawn as `Normal` |
| `render.rotate-invalid` | Warning | A `/Rotate` not a multiple of 90, read as 0 |
| `render.appearance-missing`, `render.appearance-generated` | Information | An annotation without an appearance, skipped or generated in memory |
| `render.raster-too-large`, `render.layer-budget` | Warning | A limit reached; the option that lifts it is named |

### The command-line tool

`render FILE [--pages 1-3,7] [--dpi 150 | --fit 256x256] [--box crop|media|trim|bleed|art] [--intent view|print]
[--annotations all|none] [--generate-appearances] [--format png|jpeg|webp] [--quality 85] [--out DIR]`, one file per
page, named by page index and, where the document has them, page label. `compare --visual` rasterises through the
satellite when it is present. The AOT binary ships SkiaSharp's native library beside it; its size is measured and
recorded.

## Slices

Each slice ends on a green commit, with its codes documented, its benchmark recorded in `docs/status.md` when it
has one, and every referee in a container ([ADR 27](../adr/0027-integration-tests-run-the-referees-in-containers.md)).

1. **The satellite, the seams and the page frame.** Delivers the package with its API baseline and an AOT and trimming
   check that loads the native library; `IPdfContentDevice` public, reviewed as API, M15's devices unchanged;
   `PdfRasteriser` implementing the core's `IPdfPageRasteriser`; the page frame (box, `/Rotate`, `UserUnit`,
   background); filled and stroked paths in the device spaces; PNG out; the referee harness — MuPDF (`mutool draw`)
   and poppler (`pdftoppm`) rendering in a pinned image, Pillow and NumPy comparing inside it, the side-by-side report
   as a CI artifact; **the scan threshold fixed and recorded** beside M9's and M12's (below). *Proved by* unit tests
   (the device matrix for each rotation and box, an inverted box, `UserUnit` 0.88; FsCheck — any composition of
   rotations keeps the raster's area, and rendering a page in bands gives the rows rendering it whole gives);
   integration: for every committed page, our raster's width and height equal MuPDF's at 72 and 150 dpi; the Swiss
   QR-bill's code, drawn as vector rectangles, decodes with ZXing from our raster to the payload it decodes from
   MuPDF's. *Leaves* the full line state.
2. **Paths, strokes and clips in full.** Delivers dashes, caps, joins, the miter limit, stroke adjustment,
   zero-width lines, the non-uniform pen, even-odd and non-zero clips, clip nesting through `q`/`Q`. *Proved by*
   unit tests on fixture pages built with M8's builder, one per operator and state entry; integration: the fixture
   pages and the vector-only regions of the JasperReports statement's table grid within the cross-engine threshold
   of MuPDF. *Leaves* text.
3. **Glyph outlines and text.** Delivers the outline sink for TrueType, CFF, CID-keyed CFF and Type 1, the eight
   rendering modes, clip by text, substitution for non-embedded fonts, the glyph cache, Type 3 glyphs through the
   interpreter. *Proved by* unit tests (a composite glyph with scaled components, `seac`, flex, a charstring bomb
   stopped by M8's guard, a Type 3 font that shows itself cut and reported, a font program that lies about its
   glyph count); integration: every page of the committed invoices, reports and contract within the cross-engine
   threshold of MuPDF; PDF24's boxes where MuPDF draws them; the OCR layers of the Acrobat, FineReader and Xerox
   scans absent from the raster — the page renders as it does with its text removed by M19's pipeline. *Leaves*
   images.
4. **Images and the decoders.** Delivers M22's decoders behind the device, stencil masks, `/Decode`, explicit and
   colour-key masks, soft masks, `/SMaskInData`, `/Matte`, smoothing, decode-time reduction, the image cache.
   *Proved by* unit tests (a 1 × 1 image stretched over a page, a mask whose size differs from its image's, an
   `/Indexed` lookup out of range, an image whose `/Height` contradicts its data); integration: each committed scan
   rendered **at its native resolution**, so that one sample maps to one pixel, against MuPDF at the same
   resolution under the scan threshold; the 35,000-pixel CCITT page within its memory budget. *Leaves* colour.
5. **Colour spaces, functions and shadings.** Delivers the converter seam over M22's evaluation, the seven shading
   types, tiling patterns coloured and uncoloured. *Proved by* unit tests (each shading type on fixtures; a type 4
   function at its stack bound; `Separation /All` and `/None`; a pattern cell smaller than a pixel); integration:
   the shading and pattern fixtures (**not in the corpus**, below) and the Illustrator publication's separation
   colours within the cross-engine threshold of MuPDF run without colour management (`mutool draw -N`), so that
   both sides approximate. *Leaves* transparency.
6. **Transparency.** Delivers constant alpha, the blend modes, isolated and non-isolated groups, soft masks by
   luminosity and alpha with backdrop and transfer, page groups, the knockout approximation, the layer budget.
   *Proved by* unit tests (a soft mask inside a soft mask; a group whose `/BBox` is empty; the sixteen modes over a
   known backdrop against the formulas of §11.3.5, within one step); integration: the committed documents with

### The thresholds

Two are used as they stand, already recorded in `docs/status.md`; slice 1 fixes the third on the corpus:

- **Cross-engine** — ours against MuPDF, poppler or pdf.js: M9's, unchanged — at 150 dpi, a pixel differs when a
  channel differs by more than 24 of 255 **and** no pixel within one pixel of it in the other raster matches it;
  two renderings agree when at most 1 % of the pixels differ and no 8-connected region of differing pixels
  exceeds 144 pixels, a 2 mm square.
- **Regression** — ours against our own approved image: M12's, unchanged — 0.2 % and the same region clause, with
  no neighbourhood allowance: the same engine should draw the same edges.
- **Scans at native resolution**, new here — at most 0.1 % of pixels differ, no region over 16 pixels: with one
  sample per pixel, two correct decoders and placers agree almost exactly.

Approval follows M12's workflow: references in `tests/visual/rendering/<document>/page-NNN.png`, each approved by a
reviewed commit after a person compared ours, MuPDF's and poppler's side by side, the reason for any visible
difference written in `tests/visual/rendering/APPROVALS.md`; regeneration explicit, never automatic.

## Traps

- **Hidden content still changes the state.** The interpreter runs it; the device drops its paints. Skipping a
  hidden section wholesale breaks every page that sets a colour or a font inside one.
- **An OCR layer is invisible by mode 3, or by being painted under the image.** Both must stay invisible, and the
  second only works if images are drawn in content order over it.
- **The `CropBox` and `/Rotate` are inherited**, like `Resources` and `MediaBox`: read them through inherited
  resolution, never from the page dictionary.
- **A zero-width line is not invisible**: it is the thinnest line the device draws.
- **Knockout, overprint, halftones and transfer functions** are device concepts; screen viewers ignore most of them,
  and so do we, saying so. Faking overprint in RGB makes more pages wrong than right.
- **Decoding a scan whole to draw it small** is how a thumbnail takes gigabytes. Reduce while decoding.
- **Native font parsers are an attack surface**, and system font managers make rendering machine-dependent.
  Outlines are ours; Skia's font manager is never created.
- **Anti-aliasing seams**: adjacent vector rectangles (a QR code, a table grid) show hairline gaps when each is
  anti-aliased on its own. Stroke adjustment and pixel snapping of axis-aligned fills are what keep a QR code
  decodable from a raster.
- **Skia's SIMD paths differ across processors.** Byte identity holds per processor family, not across them; the
  documentation must not promise more.
- **CMYK without a profile is an approximation**, and MuPDF with colour management on draws CMYK differently from
  any formula: compare with `-N`, and leave colour fidelity to M29.
- **`/Interpolate` is a hint.** Viewers differ on it; compare scans at native resolution, where it changes nothing.
- **pdf.js applies usage directly** where the specification applies it only through `/AS` (M11's trap): where they
  disagree, follow the specification and record the difference.
- **A render is not a repair.** PDF24's box, a missing font's substitute, an image that cannot be decoded — draw
  what the file says, report what could not be drawn, invent nothing.

- [ ] The satellite renders every page of every committed document, with the device seam public, and implements
      the core's `IPdfPageRasteriser` for M22's OCR and M24's comparison.
- [ ] Glyphs come from the core's outlines, and no font program reaches a native parser.
- [ ] Images decode through M22, reduced while decoded; colour through M22's evaluation behind the converter seam.
- [ ] Shadings, patterns, transparency, intents, layers and annotations render as the acceptance conditions say.
- [ ] Band rendering holds memory to the band; the limits are options with their codes.
- [ ] The scan threshold is fixed and recorded beside M9's and M12's; the reference images approved and
      recorded in `APPROVALS.md`.
- [ ] M24's visual rows pass on our rasters; `compare --visual` works without a caller's rasteriser.
- [ ] The priority-1 gaps above are filled; each remaining gap is recorded in `docs/corpus-contributions.md`.
- [ ] The acceptance conditions above pass on the corpus, in CI, with no document skipped, and the remote rows on a
      green `Remote corpus` run recorded in `docs/status.md`.
- [ ] Unit tests cover each behaviour, its degenerate cases and its hostile ones; the FsCheck properties hold.
- [ ] Integration tests run MuPDF, poppler, pdf.js, Pillow, NumPy and ZXing, each in a container.
- [ ] `RenderBenchmarks` runs with `MemoryDiagnoser`; `docs/status.md` records the budgets and the throughput.
- [ ] The `render` verb ships in the dotnet tool and the AOT binary, documented.
- [ ] The documentation site publishes the pages listed above.
