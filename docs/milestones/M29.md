# M29 — Print production

> State: to do — Depends on: M20, M22 — Conformance preserved or its loss reported, per [ADR 17](../adr/0017-conformance-actively-preserved-plus-a-built-in-validator.md); profiles in the conformance satellite, per [ADR 36](../adr/0036-validation-lives-in-the-core-conformance-in-a-satellite.md); a dependency-free core, per [ADR 9](../adr/0009-a-dependency-free-core-plus-satellites.md)

## Goal

Hand a print shop what it expects: invoices, statements and reports generated as PDF/X-4 with their bleed, marks
and output intent, whole batch runs as PDF/VT with one document part per record, colours converted through a
managed colour-management engine, CMYK and spot colours written from CSS, and overprint where it is asked for.

The business case is the outsourced print run: thousands of invoices or statements sent to an *éditique*
provider who prints and posts them. Such providers ask for PDF/X or PDF/VT, and M12's batch generation already
produces the records; this milestone makes the file one they accept.

## Scope

**In**: PDF/X-4 generation (output intent, trim and bleed boxes, no encryption, transparency kept live, fonts
embedded); PDF/VT-1 and PDF/VT-2 on M12.6's batch generation — document part hierarchy, record metadata, XObjects
reused across records; a PDF/X and PDF/VT profile for the M2 engine in the `AdCodicem.Pdf.Conformance` satellite;
a managed colour-management engine in a satellite of its own — ICC profiles read, conversions between them at the
four rendering intents, black-point compensation —, deterministic to the byte; CMYK from CSS (`device-cmyk()`),
spot colours through a vendor extension written as `Separation` colour spaces with their alternate, overprint;
`@page` `bleed` and `marks`; the output intent reconciled when documents with different intents are merged.

**Out, explicitly**: mixed raster content compression (an open question of the roadmap); trapping, OPI and
halftone authoring (read, preserved, never written); transparency flattening, which needs rasterisation and is not
asked of PDF/X-4; PDF/X-6 until a print provider asks for it; imposition beyond M9's N-up.

## Design

```
PdfXOptions                  the PDF/X part, the output intent's profile, bleed, marks, and the policy for
                             content that PDF/X forbids (report, or refuse)
PdfVtOptions                 the document part hierarchy, record metadata, and the reuse policy for XObjects
ColourManagement (satellite) ICC v2 and v4 profiles, transforms with a rendering intent, a transform cache;
                             no native code, no floating-point path that differs between platforms
```

- **Colour conversion is deterministic.** The same profile and the same input give the same bytes on every
  platform: fixed-point or carefully ordered arithmetic, tested across operating systems in CI.
- **PDF/VT reuses, it does not repeat.** A logo drawn on every record is one XObject referenced by every page;
  the batch generation of M12.6 already caches it, and PDF/VT records its reuse.
- **The referee.** No open-source PDF/X or PDF/VT validator is known to exist: veraPDF validates PDF/A, PDF/UA
  and WTPDF only. Choosing an independent check is this milestone's first slice (see Traps).

## Slices

1. **The referee**: decide what independently checks PDF/X-4 and PDF/VT — a commercial preflight run under a
   licence the project can use in CI or run by the maintainer with its report committed, the Ghent Workgroup's
   published test suites, or both — and record it as an ADR.
2. **Page boxes and output intents**: trim, bleed and art boxes written from `@page` `bleed`; printer's marks from
   `marks`; the output intent embedded with its profile, and reconciled on merge.
3. **The colour-management satellite**: ICC profile parsing (bounded, hostile input), transforms at each
   rendering intent, black-point compensation; tested against the reference values of the ICC's own test
   profiles.
4. **CMYK and spot colours from CSS**: `device-cmyk()`, the vendor extension for spot colours, `Separation`
   and `DeviceN` written with their alternates; overprint through the graphics state.
5. **PDF/X-4 generation**: the constraints enforced on write, and anything forbidden either converted, refused or
   reported, by policy.
6. **The PDF/X profile** in the conformance satellite, run on the corpus's PDF/X claims.
7. **PDF/VT** on the batch generation: document parts per record, record metadata, XObject reuse.
8. **The PDF/VT profile**, and the documentation.

## Tests required

**Unit** — each PDF/X constraint on write, alone; box inheritance and validity (trim within bleed within media);
colour transforms against reference values, per rendering intent, and the same bytes on each operating system;
spot colours with their alternates; the output intent reconciliation on merge; PDF/VT part hierarchy and reuse
counts; hostile ICC profiles (truncated tags, huge tag counts, recursive lookups) bounded.

**Integration** — the referee chosen in slice 1 on every PDF/X and PDF/VT document generated; veraPDF still upholds
any PDF/A claim the same documents make; qpdf `--check` on every output.

## Corpus

### What the corpus holds

A PDF/X-3 claim with a CMYK output intent from Photoshop (remote), and a PDF/A document from Distiller with a CMYK
output intent (committed). The generated reference documents of M12, and M12.6's batch of invoices.

### What it lacks

| Need | Why | Priority | Likely source |
|---|---|---|---|
| PDF/X-4 files from InDesign or Acrobat, with a preflight report | The PDF/X profile needs third-party files to agree with | 1 | A public source (the Ghent Workgroup's test suites, licence to check) or a contribution |
| A PDF/VT file from a variable-data tool | PDF/VT reading and the profile need a real one | 1 | A public source or a contribution from a print provider |
| Documents with spot colours (`Separation`, `DeviceN`) | Spot colours must be preserved on merge and written from CSS | 2 | Generated here (LibreOffice or ReportLab), and a contribution |
| An invoice run as a print provider receives it | The use case the milestone exists for | 2 | A contribution |

## Traps

- **The referee may not be free.** veraPDF does not validate PDF/X or PDF/VT, and no open-source validator is
  known; a milestone whose acceptance cannot run in CI is not specified. Slice 1 settles it before anything else.
- PDF/X-4 keeps transparency live; PDF/X-1a and X-3 forbid it. The parts differ more than their names suggest.
- A colour engine whose results differ in the last bit between platforms breaks invariant 6; floating point is
  the trap.
- An ICC profile is hostile input like any other: tag tables, counts and offsets are bounded (invariant 4).
- Spot colour names are case- and space-sensitive, and printers match them literally.
