# M18 — Legal case files

> State: to do — Depends on: M7, M9, M12, M14, M15, M16 — The `AdCodicem.Pdf.CaseFile` satellite ([ADR 9](../adr/0009-a-dependency-free-core-plus-satellites.md), [ADR 24](../adr/0024-package-identifiers-and-a-reserved-prefix.md)); every resource through the resolver of [ADR 38](../adr/0038-the-html-engine-loads-resources-deny-by-default.md); the inventory's page numbers by the passes of [ADR 39](../adr/0039-forward-references-late-filled-xobjects-and-two-bounded-passes.md)

## Goal

Build a case file from one model — numbered pieces, their titles, dates and sources — and get from it the
exhibit stamps, the bookmarks, the page labels, a hyperlinked *bordereau de communication de pièces*, a JSON
export and an integrity manifest, all of which agree because none of them is written by hand; shape the result
for the court portal it is going to, by rules kept as data and read from the official texts; turn e-mails into
pieces with their attachments; and link every "pièce n° 12" in the pleadings to the piece it names.

M6 and M7 assemble and split; M9 stamps "Pièce n° 2.1" on a page it is told to stamp. None of them knows what a
case file is. French practice does: every piece a party relies on is numbered and communicated with a bordereau
(Code de procédure civile, art. 132; a *bordereau récapitulatif* annexed to appeal conclusions, art. 954); before
the administrative courts an *inventaire détaillé* accompanies the pieces (Code de justice administrative,
art. R. 412-2), and in Télérecours each piece must be a file of its own or be identified by a bookmark designating
it as the inventory does (art. R. 414-3) — on which the Conseil d'État ruled in *SA Finamur* (5 October 2018).
Those texts are cited here as the survey reports them; each is read in its current wording before a preset
encodes it. The failures this milestone exists to prevent are the ordinary ones: a bookmark reading "Pièce 12"
while the inventory reads "Pièce 12 — Lettre de résiliation du 3 mars 2025"; a piece inserted after
communication that renumbers the stamps and not the bordereau; an inventory whose page numbers are all off by one
because the inventory itself grew a page; an e-mail printed from Outlook with its attachments gone; a file a
portal refuses for its size on the evening of a deadline; a fingerprint nobody can recompute.

## Scope

**In**:

- the **`AdCodicem.Pdf.CaseFile` satellite**, on `AdCodicem.Pdf` and `AdCodicem.Pdf.Html`, as `docs/architecture.md`
  places it;
- the **case-file model**: the matter, the communicating party and its counsel, successive communications, and
  pieces — hierarchical numbers with the *bis*, *ter* suffixes French practice inserts, titles, dates, sources
  (a PDF, an image, an HTML fragment, an e-mail), sub-pieces —, with a versioned JSON form and its published
  schema;
- **one resolution** of the model from which every product follows: M9's exhibit stamps and separator pages, M7's
  bookmarks, M6's page labels and named destinations, the bordereau rendered by M12, its links, the JSON export,
  the integrity manifest, the document metadata — and a verifier that checks each of them against the model by
  extraction;
- **outputs**: one volume, bordereau first; one file per piece and the bordereau as a file of its own; or both;
- the **bordereau**: HTML written by the satellite from the resolved model, rendered by M12, tagged by M13, its
  lines linked to the pieces; its page column filled by ADR 39's bounded passes where the bordereau's own length
  shifts what it lists;
- **signed, certified, encrypted and damaged pieces** handled by M6, M9 and M16's policies, the signed original
  kept verifiable;
- an **integrity manifest**: SHA-256 of every piece's source as received and of every file produced, embedded as
  an associated file, summarised in a custom XMP schema (M14), the volume's own digest in an appended update, and
  a sidecar;
- **court-portal presets** — Télérecours, e-Barreau and the RPVA, PLEX — as **data**: a fixed vocabulary of rules
  in code, their values in JSON with the source each was read from, each preset **verified against the current
  official texts and guides before it is encoded**, as M10 and M14 do for their specifications;
- **e-mail to a piece**: EML (RFC 5322 and MIME) and Outlook MSG (MS-OXMSG over MS-CFB), a header block and the
  body rendered through M12, inline images from the message itself, attachments as associated files or as
  sub-pieces, nested messages as sub-pieces, the original message kept as the piece's source;
- **references to pieces** — "pièce n° 12", "pièces nos 3 à 5", "(pièce 2.1)" — found by M15's search in the
  pleadings and linked (M11) to the piece's first page, in the volume or across files;
- the command-line tool's `casefile` verb.

**Out, explicitly**:

- **redacting and sanitising pieces** before they enter a case file — **M19**, which adds its steps to the piece
  pipeline defined here; until then a caller passes a source it has already processed;
- **OCR** of scanned pieces, which a portal may require to be searchable — **M22**; M18 reports each page without
  text (M15's page kinds) under the preset's rule;
- **reducing a piece's size** beyond splitting it — recompression and downsampling are **M23**'s; until then a
  piece over a cap is split when the preset allows it and reported otherwise;
- **converting pieces to PDF/A** — **M21**, through the tool's `casefile --pdf-a`, so that this satellite never
  depends on `AdCodicem.Pdf.Conformance`; checking a PDF/A claim beyond reading it — **M20**; a preset that requires
  PDF/A reports every piece whose claim is absent, and says it could not validate the others — once M20 exists,
  it asks the core's `IPdfConformanceChecker` when the caller has registered one, still without that dependency;
- **signing or sealing** the volume, the manifest or a piece, and time-stamping — **M26**; the manifest is shaped so
  that a signature over the volume binds it. No claim is made about the *copie fiable* regime of décret n° 2016-1673:
  digests make a change detectable, they do not certify a process;
- **Word and office documents as pieces** — **M31** for DOCX; the others are an open question of the roadmap;
- **sending** anything to a court or through the RPVA — **never**: the library produces files; it holds no
  credential, opens no connection and talks to no court system;
- **mailbox formats** (MBOX, PST, EML collections), S/MIME decryption, DKIM verification — not planned; the EA-PDF
  archival profile of the PDF Association (2025) — not planned here, though the rendition leaves room for it
  (see Traps);
- **presets for other jurisdictions** — the vocabulary accepts them as data; only the three French ones ship,
  verified;
- an **authored portfolio** as the output — not planned (M7 unpacks received ones).

## Design

### Where it lives

```
AdCodicem.Pdf.CaseFile
  Model/        CaseFileModel, CasePiece, CaseDate, CaseMatter, CaseCommunication, CasePieceSource,
                CaseFileJson and its schema
  Build/        CaseFileBuilder, ResolvedCaseFile, the page-label and bookmark plans, the passes
  Inventory/    CaseInventoryWriter (HTML), the default stylesheet, CaseFileLocale (fr-FR shipped)
  Integrity/    CaseIntegrityManifest, the XMP schema, the volume digest
  Portals/      CasePortalPreset, the rule vocabulary, the shipped presets as embedded JSON
  Email/        the MIME reader, the CFB and MS-OXMSG reader, EmailMessage, EmailToPiece
  References/   CasePieceReferenceFinder, CasePieceLinker, the reference patterns as data
  Verify/       CaseFileVerifier
```

The satellite adds no dependency beyond the two packages it builds on. Its parsers — MIME, compound files, LZFu,
RTF de-encapsulation — are **written here**, bounded and fuzzed like the core's, unless an ADR in slice 9 finds
that a maintained library (MimeKit for MIME, MsgReader for MSG, both MIT) meets the same bar: Native AOT and
trimming without warnings, deterministic output, and every limit below enforceable from outside. Taking one
amends the package table of `docs/architecture.md`.

Every operation that reads or writes takes M3's `IProgress<PdfProgress>` then `CancellationToken`, with the stages
`Analysing`, `Rendering`, `Assembling`, `Verifying`, counted in pieces; every result carries a report of M6's
shape.

### The model

```
CaseFileModel        immutable: Matter, Party, Counsel, Communications (ordered), Pieces (ordered, a tree),
                     Numbering, Locale, Metadata overrides; With… methods for edits
CaseMatter           court, chamber, case numbers, the parties as the caller names them — ordered label/value lines,
                     shown by the bordereau's header as written
CaseCommunication    number (1, 2, …), date (supplied, never the clock), whether it is a récapitulatif
CasePiece            Number (M9's PdfExhibitNumber, below), Title (the libellé), Date, Source, Communication,
                     Children, Notes, Tags
CaseDate             Day, Month (a month and a year), Period, or Text as written; formatted by the locale
CasePieceSource      Pdf(path | stream factory | document, credentials), Image(path | stream) → M7,
                     Html(HtmlSource) → M12, Email(eml | msg) → below, Part(an M6 assembly source)
CaseFileJson         Read(Utf8JsonReader) and Write(Utf8JsonWriter); a versioned schema published on the site
```

- **Numbers are the parties' contract.** Once a communication is marked sent, the numbers of its pieces are frozen:
  a model that renumbers them is refused, typed (`CaseFileException`, naming the piece and the communication),
  since the court and the other side cite them. A piece inserted later takes the next number or a suffix
  (`InsertPolicy.Suffix` gives "12 bis"), as the caller chooses; a *récapitulatif* lists every piece of every
  communication.
- **Numbering** is `Explicit` (the model's numbers, checked unique and ordered), `Sequential` or `Hierarchical`
  (sub-pieces numbered n.1, n.2), continued across communications.
- **One number model.** A piece's number is M9's `PdfExhibitNumber` — the value M9's stamps print through their
  `{piece}` field and M18's reference finder parses — with its components (2, 2.1, 2.1.3), an optional ordinal
  suffix (bis, ter, quater … up to the Latin ordinals it lists as data) and a total order, 12 < 12.1 < 12.2 <
  12 bis < 12 ter < 13, an inserted piece following its neighbour's sub-pieces; `Parse` is total. The suffix and
  its order belong to the core type rather than to a second model here, since a caller stamping with M9 alone numbers
  exhibits as a case file does, and two models of one number would disagree on its order: M9's specification carries
  them, and if M9 closed without them, slice 1 adds them to `PdfExhibitNumber` in the core.
- **The JSON form** is the model and nothing else — no paths resolved, no digests —, so that a caller can keep it
  under version control; the resolved form is the export (below). Reading it is hostile input: `Utf8JsonReader` at
  its default depth, strings bounded by the stream, unknown properties refused with their path.
- **Locale data is ours.** The strings of the bordereau and the stamps — "Bordereau de communication de pièces",
  "Pièce n°", "Désignation", month and day names — are data in `CaseFileLocale`, not `CultureInfo` lookups: under
  `InvariantGlobalization`, which containers and AOT deployments commonly set, `CultureInfo("fr-FR")` gives no French
  month names, and ICU's data differs between distributions (the trap M15 records for folding). fr-FR ships; other
  locales are data.

### One resolution, every product

`CaseFileBuilder.Analyse(model, options)` opens each source in turn — one open at a time — and produces a
`ResolvedCaseFile`: every piece with its page count, its page range in the volume, its labels, its bookmark title,
its file name, its source digest and what its source carries (signatures, encryption, text or not, active content,
from M4, M16 and M15's inventory). `Write(resolved, output)` then produces everything from that one object:

| Product | From the resolved model | Written through |
|---|---|---|
| Exhibit stamp and per-piece page numbers | Number, the stamp template, the piece's page range | M9's `PdfExhibitStamper`, pieces as ranges of the volume or as documents |
| Separator page | Number and title | M9's `PdfSeparatorPage`; labelled with the piece's prefix alone, so its pages start at "-1" |
| Bookmark | The title pattern (the preset's or the default "Pièce n° {number} — {title}"); sub-pieces nested; the piece's own outline nested beneath it (M6's `Nest`) or dropped | M7's `PdfOutline` |
| Named destination | `piece-{number}`, one per piece, at the top of its first page | M6's name tree |
| Page labels | The plan: bordereau in lower roman, each piece `{number}-` from 1 (default), continuous, or Bates-mirrored (M9) | M6's `PdfPageLabels` |
| Bordereau | Every line, and the numbers its page column shows | M12, from HTML the satellite writes |
| Links from the bordereau | Each line to `piece-{number}` in a volume, or a `GoToR` to the piece's file and its named destination | M12.6 and M7's open parameters |
| Metadata and viewer settings | Title from the locale and the matter, language, the dates the caller supplies; `UseOutlines`, `DisplayDocTitle` | M14's `PdfDocumentMetadata`, M6's viewer preferences |
| Integrity manifest | Every digest | M6's attachments, M14's XMP |
| JSON export | The resolved model | `CaseFileJson.WriteResolved` |
| Export anchors | Piece numbers per page | M15's `PdfAnchorProvider`, so that chunks of a volume cite "pièce 2.1, p. 3" |

No product reads another product: the bookmark title is not taken from the inventory's text, nor the stamp from the
bookmark. That is what "cannot disagree" means in code; the verifier (below) is what proves it on the output.

### Outputs

- **A volume**: M6's assembly, bordereau first (its own part, `RenderToPart`), then each piece, optionally preceded by
  its separator. The whole is one forward-only write; memory follows the heaviest page plus the resolved model, a few
  hundred bytes a piece.
- **One file per piece**: each piece its own assembly — stamps, its bookmark, its labels, the manifest entry for its
  source —, named by the preset's pattern; sub-pieces in their parent's file or in files of their own, as the preset
  or the options say. The files are written first, through a hashing sink, so that the **bordereau, written last,
  can list each file's SHA-256** and link to each by `GoToR`. A file over the preset's size rule is cut by M7's
  split by size, when the preset allows a piece in several files, and named "{number} ({k}/{n})".
- **Both**, from one analysis.
- File names follow M7's safe naming (reserved characters removed, NFC, bounded length) and then the preset's
  pattern, character set and length; accented letters transliterated to ASCII only when the preset's character set
  demands it, through M15's generated decomposition tables, never through the platform's culture.

### The bordereau

- **HTML written by the satellite**, never concatenated from strings: `CaseInventoryWriter` escapes every value of
  the model, so that a title containing `</td>` or `<img src=…>` is text. The default stylesheet — A4, the matter in
  a running header, "page X sur Y" in the footer through M12's late-filled counters — is replaceable, and so are a
  header and a footer fragment (a firm's letterhead, a signature block), loaded through the resolver of ADR 38 from
  the bases the caller declares.
- **Columns**: number, title, date, and optionally the pages, the communication, the source's or the file's SHA-256,
  and "original signé joint" where a signed original travels with the piece. Sub-pieces are indented.
- **Tagged** by M13 (`Table`, `TH` with `Scope`, `H1`, `Lang fr`), PDF/UA-1 as its target: with the e-mail
  renditions, the bordereau is what the library itself writes into a case file, and it is accessible. Whether the
  volume keeps a claim is M6's structure policy, which removes it as soon as one piece is untagged, and says so.
- **Links**: `href="#piece-2.1"` names a destination the enclosing assembly defines, not an id in the HTML. M12.6
  resolves `#id` against the document it lays out; M18 adds `PdfRenderOptions.ExternalDestinations` to
  `AdCodicem.Pdf.Html` — the member M12 reserves for this milestone —, the destinations the caller declares will
  exist, each resolved by the enclosing assembly and reported by M6 if it never appears.
  In per-piece output the links are `GoToR` to the file with `#nameddest=piece-2.1`, through M7's open parameters.

### The bordereau's page column: two bounded passes

What the page column shows decides whether the bordereau depends on its own length:

| Page plan | The column shows | Depends on the bordereau's length |
|---|---|---|
| Bordereau in roman, each piece labelled from its own start (default) | Nothing by default — every piece starts at "-1", and the link is the navigation | No: laid out once |
| Bordereau in roman, pieces numbered continuously from 1 | The label of each piece's first page | No: laid out once |
| Continuous numbering including the bordereau, or no labels (the viewer's physical page) | The physical page | **Yes** |
| Bates-mirrored labels starting on the bordereau | The Bates number | **Yes** |

In the last two rows the builder runs ADR 39's strategy itself, since the pieces are PDF parts M12 does not lay out
and `target-counter()` cannot reach them: a first render lays the bordereau out with every number replaced by a
placeholder of the reserved width (`ReservedCounterDigits` of the widest digit, `tabular-nums`), writes nothing and
keeps only its page count; the builder then knows every piece's first page and renders again, writing. A reserved
width that holds cannot change the wrapping of a line, so the page count holds; if it moves anyway — a number wider
than the reserve —, the builder renders again, up to M12's `MaxLayoutPasses`, then writes the last pass and reports
`casefile.inventory-unsettled` with the pieces whose numbers may be wrong. A measure-only render is the map-only
pass M12's orchestrator already runs on a non-seekable output; M12 exposes no call for it, so M18 adds one to `.Html`
beside `ExternalDestinations` — `IPdfRenderer.MeasureAsync(HtmlSource, …)`, returning the page count and the page of
each id, writing nothing — with its tests in M12's suites.

### Signed, certified, encrypted and damaged pieces

| Piece | In a volume | In its own file |
|---|---|---|
| Approval-signed | M6's `AttachOriginal`, the default here: the stamped copy in the volume, the signed original as a page-level associated file (`Source`) on its first page, its digest in the manifest, "original signé joint" in the bordereau | `StampAfterSignature`, the default here: M9's incremental stamp, which leaves every signature covering its revision and reports `stamp.after-signature`; or the original passed through byte for byte, unstamped, when the caller or the preset prefers |
| Certified (`DocMDP`) | M6's `Refuse`, by default; `Insist` treats it as signed and reports the certification voided in the copy — the original travels intact beside it | Refused by M9; passed through unstamped when the caller asks |
| Encrypted | Opened with the source's `PdfCredentials` (M16); `Assemble` demanded under `PdfPermissionPolicy.Respect`, as M16's table says; the output unencrypted unless the options encrypt it | Likewise |
| Damaged | Read by the tolerant reader, optionally repaired first (M5); every loss M1 and M5 report is carried into the piece's report, never silent | Likewise |
| Without text | Assembled; reported per page under the preset's text rule, if it has one | Likewise |
| With active content | Assembled as it is (M6 keeps scripts); reported under the preset's active-content rule; removed by M19 once it exists | Likewise |

### The integrity manifest

- **What is hashed.** Each piece's **source as received** — the bytes of the PDF, image or message file, streamed
  through `SHA256` —; each **file produced** in per-piece output; the **volume**. Sub-pieces extracted from an e-mail
  are hashed as the bytes the message carried, after transfer decoding.
- **The manifest** is a JSON document — algorithm, matter reference, generator name and version, each piece's number,
  title, source name, media type, length and digest, its page range, its output file's name and digest where there
  is one — serialised deterministically (fixed property order, invariant numbers, no insignificant whitespace), under
  a published schema. It is embedded in the volume as a document-level associated file, `AFRelationship /Supplement`,
  `application/json`, and written beside it as a sidecar.
- **XMP**: a custom schema of M14's — a namespace URI under the project's control, fixed before the first stable
  release, prefix `adcCase` — carries the matter reference, the communication, the piece count and the manifest's
  own digest, with its extension schema description generated for PDF/A.
- **The volume's digest cannot sit inside the bytes it digests.** It is written in an **appended update**: revision 1
  is the volume, written through a hashing sink; revision 2 changes only the metadata stream, adding
  `adcCase:volumeDigest`, the SHA-256 of revision 1's bytes — the file up to the end M4's revision history gives
  revision 1. Anyone can recompute it with M4's `PdfRevision.CopyTo`, pyHanko's revision extraction or `head -c`.
  Forward-only, no placeholder patched; the update needs the writer's index of revision 1, which it still holds, and
  an output it can append to. A non-seekable output, a preset that forbids incremental updates, or the caller selects
  `VolumeDigest.SidecarOnly`, and the report says where the digest went. A later signature (M26) covers both
  revisions.
- **Recomputation** is `CaseFileVerifier`'s: a piece's digest that no longer matches its source names the piece.

### Court-portal presets, as data

A preset is a JSON document shipped as an embedded resource:

```
CasePortalPreset     id, name, jurisdiction, version; Sources: [{ url, title, clause, retrieved, verifiedOn }];
                     Rules: [{ rule, value, source }]
CasePortalRule       one class per rule of the vocabulary: Check(resolved, outputs) -> findings; Shape(plan)
```

| Rule | Checks | Shapes by |
|---|---|---|
| `portal.layout` | One file per piece, one file with a bookmark per piece, or either | Choosing the output |
| `portal.bookmark-title` | Each piece's bookmark title against the pattern, and its length | Writing it from the model |
| `portal.file-name` | Each file name against the pattern, character set and length | Naming |
| `portal.file-size` | Each file's length | M7's split by size, when `split-allowed`; otherwise a finding |
| `portal.total-size`, `portal.file-count` | The submission's sums | A finding — dividing a filing into several sendings is the caller's decision |
| `portal.inventory` | The bordereau present, where the preset wants it — first in the volume, or a file of its own | Writing it |
| `portal.text` | Every page of every piece has text (M15's page kinds) | A finding naming pieces and pages — OCR is M22's |
| `portal.encryption` | No piece or file encrypted | Writing unencrypted; an encrypted piece whose permissions forbid it is a finding |
| `portal.active-content` | No script, launch or submit action (M15's inventory) | A finding — removal is M19's |
| `portal.pdf-version` | The header version | M3's output version |
| `portal.pdfa` | A PDF/A claim on every file | A finding — validation is M20's, conversion M21's |
| `portal.page-size` | The page boxes | M9's fit to A4, when the preset allows it |
| `portal.stamp` | The exhibit stamp on each piece's first page | M9 |

- **Values are sourced or absent.** Every value cites one of the preset's sources; `CasePortalPresetTests` fails on a
  value without a source, a source without `verifiedOn`, or a rule the vocabulary does not know. **No number is
  written in this document** — no size cap, no file count: each is read from the text or guide in force when the
  preset is encoded, recorded with the preset, and cited in the commit that encodes it.
- **Shipped presets**, each verified before it is encoded:
  - `telerecours` — the administrative courts' application: the Code de justice administrative (R. 412-2 on the
    inventory, R. 414-1 and following on Télérecours, R. 414-3 on how pieces are presented), *SA Finamur* on what a
    bookmark must carry, and the application's own guide and FAQ for what the texts leave to it;
  - `rpva` — electronic communication before the judicial courts through e-Barreau and the RPVA: the Code de
    procédure civile (art. 748-1 and following) and the technical orders in force, and the platform's guide;
  - `plex` — the ministry of justice's platform for exchanging files too large for the RPVA: its conditions of use
    and guide.
- **Re-verification** is part of the release procedure (`docs/releasing.md`): a stable release re-reads every
  preset's sources and records the date. No test reads the clock.
- The vocabulary's own tests use **synthetic presets** with arbitrary values; shipped presets are tested by what they
  encode, not by numbers invented for the test.

### E-mail to a piece

```
EmailMessage            parsed: headers as written and decoded (From, Sender, To, Cc, Bcc when present, Reply-To,
                        Date, Subject, Message-ID, In-Reply-To), bodies (text, HTML, related parts), attachments
                        (name, media type, disposition, content id, length), nested messages; the source's digest
EmailReader             ReadEml(stream, EmailLimits), ReadMsg(stream, EmailLimits) -> EmailMessage + report
EmailToPiece            Render(message, EmailRenderOptions) -> a CasePieceSource (an M6 part) and its sub-pieces
EmailRenderOptions      header fields shown and their order; date display (as written, or at a fixed offset the
                        caller gives); body preference (Html, Text); attachments (AssociatedFiles, SubPieces, Both,
                        ListOnly); nested messages (SubPieces); attachment converters (a caller's map from a media
                        type to a function returning a CasePieceSource); stylesheet; fonts; conformance target (M14's
                        PDF/A-3 when attachments are embedded); limits
```

- **EML** — RFC 5322 headers, unfolded; RFC 2047 encoded words (B and Q) in any charset the framework's
  `CodePagesEncodingProvider` knows, an unknown one reported and read as UTF-8 then Latin-1; RFC 2231 parameter
  continuations and charsets for file names; base64 tolerant of line breaks and junk, quoted-printable tolerant of
  bad escapes; multipart boundaries with a missing final boundary read to the end, reported; `format=flowed`
  (RFC 3676); `message/rfc822` nested; `multipart/signed` rendered with its signature part kept as an attachment and
  reported unverified; `application/pkcs7-mime` and PGP-encrypted bodies reported unreadable; `winmail.dat` (TNEF)
  kept as an attachment and reported.
- **The rendition** is an HTML document the satellite writes and M12 renders: a header block — a labelled table,
  visibly framed, escaped, with the attachments listed by name, size and digest and where each went ("pièce 12.1",
  "fichier joint") —, then the body. An **HTML body** keeps its own styles, placed in a cascade layer below the
  header's (M12.1), so that a message cannot hide or restyle the header block; `cid:` references resolve from the
  message's own parts through a resolver over the message; `data:` is allowed; **every remote reference is refused**
  by ADR 38's default — a tracking pixel is never fetched, and each refusal is in the report. A **text body** is
  escaped into a `pre-wrap` block, flowed lines joined. Conditional comments, VML and `mso-` properties degrade as
  M12 degrades anything it does not support.
- **Attachments**: as associated files on the piece's first page (`AFRelationship` `Unspecified` by default,
  configurable), as sub-pieces — PDFs appended as 12.1, 12.2, images through M7, nested messages rendered in turn —,
  or both. What cannot become pages (a spreadsheet, an archive) is attached and listed, never opened — unless the
  caller's attachment converters name its media type: M31's DOCX conversion is the case in view, so that a Word
  attachment becomes a sub-piece while this satellite never references `AdCodicem.Pdf.Docx`. The original message is
  attached at document level with `AFRelationship /Source`; its digest is the piece's source digest.
- **Dates** are shown as written, with their offset; a normalised display uses the offset in the message or one the
  caller supplies, never the machine's zone (invariant 6). MSG carries times in UTC only (below): shown in UTC unless
  the caller gives an offset.

### MSG

- **MS-CFB**: header, FAT and DIFAT chains, the mini stream, the directory's red-black tree walked iteratively with a
  visited set; a chain longer than the file's sector count is a cycle, cut and reported.
- **MS-OXMSG**: the property streams (`__substg1.0_XXXXYYYY`, `__properties_version1.0`), recipients (type, display
  name, SMTP address — an Exchange X.500 address shown as written when no SMTP address exists), attachments
  (`PidTagAttachDataBinary`, embedded messages as sub-storages), `PidTagInternetCodepage` for 8-bit bodies.
- **Bodies**: `PidTagHtml` when present; otherwise `PidTagRtfCompressed` decompressed (MS-OXRTFCP's LZFu, its CRC
  checked, its declared size bounded) and, when it encapsulates HTML (`\fromhtml1`), de-encapsulated as MS-OXRTFEX
  describes; otherwise `PidTagBody`; a native RTF body with no plain-text twin is rendered as its text, reported
  (`email.rtf-body-as-text`).
- The roadmap calls MSG optional; it is specified here in a slice of its own, behind the same `EmailMessage`, so
  that it can be taken or deferred without touching the rest. M18 may close without it: the MSG half of the e-mail
  row and its exit criterion then move to a debt row in `docs/status.md`, naming what brings it back — a caller
  whose pieces arrive as Outlook messages.

### References to pieces

```
CasePieceReferenceFinder   Find(document, resolved, CaseReferencePatterns) -> references: text, quads, numbers, page
CasePieceLinker            Link(document, references, target: volume | per-piece files) -> M11 link annotations
CaseReferencePatterns      data: French by default — "pièce n° 12", "pièce no 12", "Pièce N° 12", "nº", "pièces nos
                           3, 4 et 7", "pièces n° 3 à 5", "(pièce 2.1)", "pièce 12 bis" — and exclusions ("pièce
                           adverse n° 4", "pièce n° 4 adverse", "pièces justificatives")
```

- Runs on M15's search — regular expressions without backtracking, folding the spaces of U+00A0 and U+202F, across
  line breaks and hyphens —, so that "pièce n°⏎12" is found with a quad per line fragment.
- A single reference is linked on its whole phrase; an enumeration or a range links each number, the prefix linking
  to the first. A number the model does not hold is reported (`casefile.reference-unknown`), never linked; an
  excluded context is reported as seen and left alone.
- **Never linked**: text inside our own exhibit stamps and Bates numbers — M9's marks are `Pagination` artifacts, which
  M15 marks as furniture —, the bordereau's own lines, text already under a link, text hidden by optional content.
- **Targets**: in a volume, a `GoTo` to `piece-{number}`; with per-piece files, a `GoToR` to the file with its named
  destination (M7). In a tagged document each link gets its `Link` element and object reference, as M11 does.

### Checking a case file

`CaseFileVerifier.Verify(output, resolved | embedded manifest, preset?)` reads what was produced and compares it with
the model, by extraction rather than by trusting the builder: the stamp text on each piece's first page and the
per-piece page numbers (M15's search), each bookmark's title and target (M7's outline), each label (M6), each line of
the bordereau and the page its link reaches (M15's text in structure order, M11's links), each digest recomputed,
each preset rule. The same verifier checks a volume the caller received, against a model the caller supplies. Its
findings are `casefile.*` codes; the `casefile check` verb prints them.

### Bounds (ADR 34, applied to the satellite)

| Bound | Kind | Why |
|---|---|---|
| MIME nesting depth, parts per message, decoded part length, unfolded header length, nested messages | **Guards**, `EmailLimits`, on by default, each reached reported as `email.limit-*` naming its property; `ThrowOnLimit` as the reader has it | A valid message can nest forwarded chains deeply, carry hundreds of parts or a header of thousands of recipients; defaults set from the corpus messages with a margin and recorded |
| An LZFu body's declared size; a CFB stream's declared size | Checked against `EmailLimits.MaxDecodedPartLength` before any allocation | Sizes come from the file |
| A CFB sector chain longer than the file's sector count | Internal: a cycle, cut | No valid file has one |
| RTF group nesting | No bound: an explicit stack, memory proportional to the input | Walked iteratively |
| Bordereau layout passes | M12's `MaxLayoutPasses` | ADR 39 |
| Reference patterns' match time | None needed: `NonBacktracking` | M15 |
| Pieces in a model | None: the model is the caller's, a few hundred bytes a piece | Not read from a hostile file |

### Report codes

`casefile.*`, `portal.*` (the rules above) and `email.*`, public and stable from the first stable release, disjoint
from reader diagnostics, validation rules and every other report family:

| Code | Severity | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `casefile.numbering-frozen` | Error (refusal) | The model renumbers a piece of a communication already sent |
| `casefile.signed-original-attached` | Information | A signed piece travels as a stamped copy with its original attached |
| `casefile.piece-without-text` | Warning | A piece has pages without text (the page kinds of M15) |
| `casefile.piece-split` | Information | A piece was cut into several files by the size rule |
| `casefile.inventory-unsettled` | Warning | The bordereau's passes ran out; the pieces named may be listed a page off |
| `casefile.reference-unknown` | Warning | A reference to a number the model does not hold |
| `casefile.reference-excluded` | Information | A reference in an excluded context, left unlinked |
| `casefile.stamp-mismatch`, `casefile.bookmark-mismatch`, `casefile.label-mismatch`, `casefile.inventory-mismatch`, `casefile.link-unresolved` | Error | The verifier found a product that disagrees with the model |
| `casefile.digest-mismatch` | Error | A recomputed digest differs from the manifest's |
| `email.charset-unknown`, `email.boundary-missing`, `email.encoding-damaged` | Warning | Read through, as described |

### The command-line tool

```
casefile build    MODEL.json -o OUT.pdf | --per-piece DIR [--preset ID] [--manifest SIDECAR.json] [--volume-digest update|sidecar]
casefile check    (VOLUME.pdf | DIR) [--model MODEL.json] [--preset ID]
casefile inventory MODEL.json -o BORDEREAU.pdf
casefile email    IN.eml|IN.msg -o OUT.pdf [--attachments embed|pieces|both|list]
casefile link     PLEADINGS.pdf --model MODEL.json -o OUT.pdf [--volume | --per-piece DIR]
casefile presets  [--json]                     each preset, its rules, its sources and when they were verified
```

Every sub-verb takes `--json` and M6's exit codes; a refusal by policy — frozen numbering, a certified piece, a
permission — is exit code 4, a verifier finding at error severity exit code 1.

## Traps

- **The bookmark must say what the inventory says.** A signet built from a different string than the inventory line —
  a short title here, the full libellé there, a non-breaking space in one and a space in the other — is the exact
  mismatch the texts sanction. One model, one string, and a verifier that compares by extraction with spaces folded.
- **Numbers are cited by others.** Renumbering after communication breaks every reference in the pleadings of both
  parties and of the court; "12 bis" exists for this reason.
- **The bordereau is part of what it counts.** Placed first, its own length moves every physical page after it; with
  labels that restart per piece nothing moves — choose the plan before choosing the algorithm.
- **"n°" has many spellings**: the degree sign, the masculine ordinal (U+00BA), a superscript "o", "no", "N°", with or
  without a space, "nos" for the plural; an OCR layer adds its own. A pattern that knows one misses half the
  references.
- **Our own stamps say "Pièce n° 12".** A linker that searches the volume finds them first.
- **A volume carries no signature.** Merging voids every signature it copies; the signed original must travel beside
  the stamped copy, and a file per piece can instead stamp after the signature.
- **A digest cannot contain itself.** The volume's digest lives in an appended update over revision 1, or beside the
  file — never "patched in" to bytes it covers.
- **Portal rules change and are not ours.** A number copied from a blog or a colleague is a rejected filing waiting to
  happen: every value cites the text or the guide it was read from, and the date.
- **E-mail is hostile HTML.** A body can restyle whatever it shares a stylesheet with, fetch a pixel that tells its
  sender the message was opened, and pretend to be a header block. The header's styles win by layer, nothing remote is
  fetched, and the header block is framed as ours.
- **MSG has no time zone.** Its times are UTC; the sender's offset is gone. Showing the machine's local time would
  make the same file render differently on two servers.
- **`CultureInfo` is not a locale database** under invariant globalisation: French month names come from the
  satellite's data.
- **An Exchange sender may have no SMTP address** — an X.500 path is what the message holds, and what is shown.
- **What would reopen EA-PDF**: an archive or a court asking for e-mail pieces in the PDF Association's archival profile;
  the rendition already keeps the source message as an associated file, which EA-PDF also asks for.

## Exit criteria

- [ ] The model, its JSON form and schema, numbering with suffixes and frozen communications exist, and every product
      follows from one resolution.
- [ ] A volume and files per piece carry stamps, bookmarks, labels, named destinations and a bordereau that agree with
      the model, confirmed by the verifier and by external extraction.
- [ ] The bordereau is rendered by M12, tagged, linked, and its page numbers are right under every page plan, within
      ADR 39's bounded passes.
- [ ] Signed pieces keep a verifiable original; certified pieces are refused unless the caller insists; encrypted and
      damaged pieces follow M16's and M5's policies, and every loss is reported.
- [ ] The integrity manifest is embedded, summarised in XMP, and every digest — the volume's included — recomputes with
      an independent script.
- [ ] The Télérecours, e-Barreau/RPVA and PLEX presets exist as data, each value citing a source read and dated, and a
      test per rule holds on the reference case file.
- [ ] EML becomes pieces with their headers, body and attachments intact, and MSG likewise — or its deferral is a
      debt row, the roadmap making it optional; nothing remote is fetched; every guard is reported under its own
      code.
- [ ] References to pieces are found and linked, and never on our own stamps.
- [ ] The acceptance conditions above pass on the corpus, in CI, with no document skipped, and the remote rows on a
      green `Remote corpus` run recorded in `status.md`.
- [ ] Unit tests cover each behaviour, its degenerate and its hostile cases; the MIME, CFB, LZFu and RTF readers join
      the nightly fuzzing campaign.
- [ ] Integration tests run poppler, qpdf, pikepdf, PyMuPDF, pdf.js, veraPDF, pyHanko, ExifTool, pdfplumber and the
      Python oracles in containers.
- [ ] `CaseFileBenchmarks` measures analysis, a hundred-piece volume and e-mail rendering with `MemoryDiagnoser`; the
      memory budget is enforced in CI and recorded in `status.md`.
- [ ] The tool's `casefile` verb ships with documented JSON.
- [ ] The documentation site publishes the pages above; `features.json` and `status.md` are brought in line.
