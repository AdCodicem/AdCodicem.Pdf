# M19 — Redaction and sanitisation

> State: to do — Depends on: M15, M16 — Active content read, reported and removed per [ADR 37](../adr/0037-out-of-scope-active-content-and-pdf-to-office.md); pixels per [ADR 42](../adr/0042-image-codecs-and-scans.md); conformance kept or its loss reported per [ADR 17](../adr/0017-conformance-actively-preserved-plus-a-built-in-validator.md); the `action.*` and `hidden.*` families in the core's engine per [ADR 36](../adr/0036-validation-lives-in-the-core-conformance-in-a-satellite.md); key types from the Arlington model per [ADR 44](../adr/0044-object-shape-rules-generated-from-the-arlington-model.md)

## Goal

Remove — not cover — what a caller marks or a detector finds: the glyphs, the vector paths and, where the core can
decode them, the image pixels under each mark, and the same text wherever else the file keeps it; report
everything a document carries beyond what its pages show, and remove it by category; always as a full rewrite, and
verified by reading the result again.

A case file leaves the office: to the other side, to the court, to a publisher of decisions, to a client's
auditor. Before it does, names are anonymised, account numbers removed, and whatever the producer left behind —
an author's account name, a designer's `C:\Users\…` path, a script that runs on opening, a comment thread, an
earlier revision — stripped. The failures this milestone exists to prevent are the famous ones: a black rectangle
drawn over text that is still there to select; a "redaction" saved as an incremental update, the removed bytes
still in the file; the OCR words of a scan removed while its pixels still show the name; a name gone from the page
and still in the bookmark, the document title, the XMP history, a figure's alternative text, the thumbnail made
before, the Factur-X XML attached to the invoice, or the font subset whose glyphs spell it; a sanitised file whose
scripts are gone but whose open action still launches a program; white text on a white page telling a language
model what to conclude.

## Scope

**In**:

- the **content-stream editing pipeline** — read, filter, rewrite — grown from M11's marked-content filter on M15's
  interpreter, shared by every later content change (M21, M23, M24's tests);
- **true redaction**: marks as `/Redact` annotations, authored, read from other tools, reviewed, then applied —
  glyphs removed without moving the rest, paths clipped, shadings and patterns confined, form XObjects edited as
  copies, annotations and fields under a mark removed or cleared, image pixels replaced where a decoder is at hand,
  an overlay drawn, the tagged structure kept coherent;
- **scrubbing beyond the page**: the removed text replaced in annotations, form values and XFA data, outline items,
  metadata (`/Info` and XMP), alternative, actual and expansion text, file names and descriptions, destination
  names; scripts that hold it removed; attachments that hold it refused, removed or kept by policy; thumbnails and
  page-piece data of marked pages removed; **font glyphs that no longer draw scrubbed** from the embedded programs;
- **always a full rewrite**: earlier revisions, unreferenced objects and trailing bytes never written; signed inputs
  refused unless the caller insists;
- **verification** after every application: the output reopened, extracted again, searched, and its decoded bytes
  scanned, the operation failing when anything survived;
- **search-and-redact**: detectors for IBAN, the French NIR, SIREN and SIRET, e-mail addresses, telephone numbers and
  dates of birth, term lists and regular expressions, and a seam for the caller's own; hits become marks to review;
- **inspect-then-sanitise**: a second built-in profile of M2's engine, `disclosure`, with the **`action.*` and
  `hidden.*` rule families**; sanitisation by category — JavaScript, automatic actions, launch, submit and import
  actions, rich media, XFA, embedded files, hidden layers, hidden annotations, invisible text, comments, form data,
  metadata, image metadata, application data, thumbnails, others' watermarks, usage rights, encryption, signatures,
  earlier revisions —, with every loss of PDF/A, PDF/UA, Factur-X or a signature reported;
- the command-line tool's `redact` and `sanitise` verbs.

**Out, explicitly**:

- **pixels of JPEG, JPEG 2000, JBIG2 and CCITT images** — **M22**, whose `AdCodicem.Pdf.Imaging` decoders and lossless
  encoders plug into the codec set defined here; until then a mark over such an image is refused or the image
  removed whole, as the caller chooses — never left under an overlay (ADR 42);
- **OCR** of a scan so that search can find text in it — **M22**; a search that finds nothing on image-only pages
  says so;
- **reflowing** text to close the gap a removal leaves — never: the library does not lay out received documents, and
  the limit this leaves is documented below;
- **rasterising a page to redact it** — not planned; M25 could offer it, at the price of the text layer;
- **named-entity recognition** — not in the library: a caller brings a model through `IPdfTextDetector`;
- **executing JavaScript**, even to learn what it does — never (ADR 37); scripts are read, reported and removed;
- **rendering or redacting dynamic XFA** — never (ADR 37): refused, and its XFA removable;
- **recursive sanitisation of embedded PDFs** — an embedded document is kept or removed; M7 unpacks a portfolio into
  one volume, which is the route to sanitising its members;
- **re-signing** a redacted document — **M26**; **converting** it to PDF/A — **M21**.

## Design

### Where it lives

In the core, with no dependency (invariant 1):

```
Content/Editing/            PdfContentEditor — M11's filter grown into the pipeline
Redaction/                  AdCodicem.Pdf.Redaction: marks, the plan, apply, the overlay, scrubbing, glyph
                            scrubbing, verification
Redaction/Detectors/        IPdfTextDetector, the built-in detectors and their data tables
Sanitisation/               AdCodicem.Pdf.Sanitisation: categories, profiles, the plan, apply
Validation/Rules/Disclosure the action.* and hidden.* rules and ValidationProfile.Disclosure
```

| Type | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| `PdfRedactor` | `Mark(document, marks) → a change set of /Redact annotations`; `Plan(document, options) → PdfRedactionPlan`; `Apply(plan, output, progress, cancellation) → PdfRedactionReport` |
| `PdfRedactionMark` | A page, quadrilaterals (M11's `PdfQuad`) or rectangles in default user space, the glyph ranges when it comes from a hit, the overlay, a kind and a confidence, an author |
| `PdfRedactionOptions` | Immutable: overlay (`Box(colour)`, `Text(text, style)`, `None`), replacement text, glyph rule, images (`Refuse`, `RemoveImage`), annotations crossing a mark (`Remove`, `Keep`), attachments (`Refuse`, `Remove`, `Keep`), scrubbing of removed text elsewhere and its minimum length, glyph scrubbing, verification (`Fail`, `Report`), signed-document and conformance policies, output version (ADR 40), document identifier, the image codec set |
| `PdfRedactionPlan` | Per mark: the glyphs, paths, images and annotations it removes and how; every place the text will be scrubbed; refusals; losses; the same text found elsewhere on the pages — inspectable before a byte is written |
| `PdfRedactionReview` | Accept or reject each mark by its `/NM` |
| `PdfRedactionReport` | Entries (code, page, object, message), exact counts by code, the verification's outcome; stable JSON |
| `IPdfTextDetector`, `PdfDetectors`, `PdfDetection` | Detection over M15's folded page text; the built-ins below |
| `PdfSanitiser` | `Inspect(document) → PdfDisclosureReport`; `Plan(document, categories, options) → PdfSanitisationPlan`; `Apply(plan, output, …) → PdfSanitisationReport` |
| `PdfSanitisationCategory`, `PdfSanitisationProfile` | Flags, and the named sets `ActiveContent`, `HiddenData`, `All` |
| `PdfImageCodecs` | The decoders and lossless encoders an operation may use: the core's — uncompressed, Flate, LZW, RunLength, the ASCII filters, predictors — by default; M22's satellite supplies the rest. An immutable value passed in options, never a static registry (invariant 8) |

Every operation takes M3's `IProgress<PdfProgress>` then `CancellationToken`, with the stages `Planning`,
`Rewriting` and `Verifying`, counted in pages. M16's permission table gains two rows: applying a redaction demands
`Modify`, and `Copy` besides when its marks come from search or a detector; sanitising demands `Modify`; removing
encryption stays owner authentication's.

### Always a full rewrite, always verified

- **An incremental update keeps what it replaces.** The bytes of every earlier revision stay in the file, readable by
  anyone who cuts it at an earlier `%%EOF` — M4 does exactly that. `Apply` therefore offers no incremental mode: the
  output is M3's full rewrite, which writes only what the current revision reaches — no earlier revision, no
  unreferenced object, no free-list remnant, no stale tail after `%%EOF`, no linearisation hint.
- **The review copy is not the redaction.** `Mark` adds annotations and removes nothing; the marked document may be
  saved in any mode, opened in Acrobat — which shows the marks and can apply them itself —, reviewed and changed. The
  report of `Mark` says it is not redacted (`redaction.marks-not-applied`), and the tool names its output `-review`.
- **The identifier does not link the copy to the original.** The output's `/ID` is derived from its own content (M3)
  or supplied by the caller; the input's is never carried.
- **Verification** is the last stage of every `Apply` (below). A redaction that cannot be verified is not reported
  done.

### The content editing pipeline

- **One pipeline.** M11 wrote the core's first content rewriter for layer removal and kept its operator table and
  state tracking apart from the layer logic for this milestone. `PdfContentEditor` runs M15's interpreter over a
  page's streams as one sequence — or over a form XObject, a Type 3 glyph procedure, an annotation appearance —
  with an `IPdfContentEdit` that decides for each operation: **keep** (its source bytes copied as they were, never
  reformatted), **drop**, or **replace** with operators written by M8's builder.
- **What an edit knows** is what the interpreter knows: the graphics and text state, each glyph's box and its source
  location — the operator's index and the glyph's index in its string, the two integers M15 keeps for this purpose.
- **Hidden content still changes state** (M11's rule): an edit that removes painting keeps `cm`, `gs`, colours,
  `q` and `Q`, clipping and text positioning.
- **Output**: a page with an edit gets one new content stream, compressed on the fly; a page without one keeps its
  streams, copied by the full rewrite as they were. A form XObject edited for one placement is written as a copy,
  so every other placement keeps the original.
- The pipeline is internal; M21 and M23 use it inside the core and M24's tests through the test support.

### What a mark removes

| Content | Wholly inside a mark | Crossing its boundary | Wholly outside |
|---|---|---|---|
| Glyphs — any render mode, visible or not, in any layer | Removed, their advance kept (below) | Removed whole when the glyph rule selects it, kept whole otherwise: **a glyph is never cut in two** | Untouched |
| A filled or stroked path | Its painting removed; its construction kept, as `n`, when it also clips | Its geometry clipped: fills subtracted from the mark, strokes cut against the mark grown by half the line width and the cap or miter extent | Untouched, byte for byte |
| A clipping path that paints nothing | Replaced by an empty rectangle at the same place, which clips as much | Kept | Untouched |
| An axial, radial or function shading (`sh`, shading patterns) | Removed | Wrapped in an even-odd clip that excludes the mark — its function is a gradient, not an image | Untouched |
| A mesh shading (types 4 to 7) | Removed | **Removed whole**, reported: a mesh can carry an image | Untouched |
| A tiling pattern fill | Removed | Clipped like a shading; the cell's own content kept and reported (`redaction.pattern-kept`) | Untouched |
| An image, XObject or inline | Pixels replaced, or the image removed | Pixels under the mark replaced (below) | Untouched |
| A form XObject | Its content edited in a copy, the mark mapped through its matrix and the CTM | Likewise | Shared, untouched |
| An annotation other than a widget | Removed, with its popup and replies (M11) | Removed by default; `Keep` leaves it and reports it | Untouched |
| A widget | Its field's value cleared and its appearance regenerated (M16), or the field removed by option | Likewise | Untouched |

- **Which glyphs.** A mark from a hit removes exactly the hit's glyphs, **and anything else inside its quads** — the
  second copy of a "fake bold" run M15 merged, a second text layer under an OCR'd image. A mark drawn by a person
  removes a glyph whose centre lies inside it or whose box overlaps it by a fraction slice 2 fixes against PyMuPDF's
  rule and records.
- **Visibility is irrelevant.** Text hidden by optional content, drawn in render mode 3 under a scan, clipped away or
  off the crop box is still text in the file: under a mark, it goes.

### Text: removing glyphs without moving the rest

- In `Tj`, `TJ`, `'` and `"`, the string is split around the removed glyphs, and each removed run becomes a number in
  a `TJ` array equal to its displacement — computed from the font's widths with the font size, character spacing,
  word spacing on the single-byte code 32 only, and horizontal scaling in force, as ISO 32000-2 §9.4.4 composes
  them, in the writing mode's direction. Every kept glyph draws where it did, to a thousandth of a point.
- `'` becomes `T*` and a `TJ`; `"` sets `Tw` and `Tc` first, as it did. A text object whose every glyph is removed
  keeps its state operators. A glyph in a clipping render mode (4 to 7) removed shrinks the clip, which hides more,
  never less.
- **What positions still say.** Bland, Iyer and Levchenko (*Story Beyond the Eye: Glyph Positions Break PDF Text
  Redaction*, PoPETs 2023) showed that the width of removed text, and the fine positioning of the text around it,
  can narrow down what was removed. The overlay's width says as much; the kept glyphs stay where they were because
  moving them would change the document. The library does not reflow; `PadOverlay` widens the overlay to whole ems
  without touching the displacement, and the limit is documented, not hidden.

### Paths, clips and shadings

- **Fills** crossing a mark are subtracted from it by polygon clipping, the fill rule (nonzero or even-odd) kept;
  curves are flattened only on paths that cross a mark, at a tolerance and within a segment budget, reported
  (`redaction.path-flattened`). Whether the clipper is written here (the Vatti algorithm) or Clipper2's C# sources
  (Boost Software License 1.0) are vendored with their notice is **decided by an ADR in slice 2**, on robustness
  measured against degenerate inputs — coincident edges, self-intersections, zero-area rings.
- **Strokes** are cut into sub-paths outside the grown mark; each piece keeps the dash pattern with its phase set to
  the arc length it started at, so that dashes outside the mark fall where they did; cut ends take the path's cap,
  which the growth keeps outside the mark.
- A path whose every point lies outside the mark is copied as its source bytes — the ordinary case, and the one that
  keeps a document's rules and backgrounds untouched.

### Images and pixels

- **Decodable** images — by the options' `PdfImageCodecs` — are decoded **a row at a time**, the mark mapped through
  the inverse of the image's placement into sample space (a polygon, for a rotated or skewed placement), every sample
  whose square intersects it replaced, and the rows re-encoded with the same filter and predictor. Nothing but the
  samples under the mark changes, and the re-encoding is lossless.
- **The value written** is uniform: the overlay's colour where the space is device grey, RGB or CMYK, or Indexed over
  one of them (the nearest palette entry); the components' minimum in any other space, which the overlay covers
  anyway. What matters is that no sample under the mark keeps its value.
- **Masks are images too**, and carry the shape of what was drawn: an `/SMask` or a `/Mask` stream is redacted under
  the same mark; an image mask's bits are set to paint nothing, through its `/Decode`; a colour-key mask gets a
  replacement value outside its ranges.
- **Shared images** are edited as copies; if the original is still shown elsewhere, the report names the pages
  (`redaction.image-shown-elsewhere`) — the caller marked a region, not the image.
- **Inline images** redacted are written as image XObjects: rewritten inline data can contain an `EI` a reader takes
  for the end. Reported.
- **Undecodable images** — DCT, JPX, JBIG2 and CCITT until M22, or any filter the codec set lacks — under
  `Refuse`, the default, are listed by the plan with their filter and the milestone that decodes them, and applying
  throws `PdfRedactionException`; under `RemoveImage` the placement is removed whole and reported
  (`redaction.image-removed`). Never an overlay over pixels that are still there.
- **M22's path**: the Imaging satellite adds its decoders and lossless encoders to `PdfImageCodecs` — a JBIG2 or
  CCITT scan re-encoded losslessly in its own filter, a DCT image re-encoded as Flate by default, or edited in the DCT
  domain with only the blocks under the mark flattened, which is M22's to decide.

### The overlay

- Drawn after the page's content, inside `q … Q` from the page's initial state (M9's balance analysis), filling each
  mark's quadrilaterals — not their union —: `/IC` (black by default), `/OverlayText` with `/DA`'s size and colour,
  `/Q` alignment and `/Repeat`, clipped to the quadrilateral and upright in the displayed orientation (M9's
  placement); an `/RO` form XObject is drawn as given.
- The face is the registry's, embedded and subset (M8); a `/DA` naming Helvetica from `/AcroForm /DR` keeps its size
  and colour and gets the registry's face, reported — never a font left unembedded. Colour and transparency follow
  M9's conformance table.
- **Tagged documents**: an overlay without text is an artifact — of type `/Pagination` with the `/Redaction` subtype
  in 2.0 output, as ISO 32000-2 §14.8.2.2.2 lists it (checked in slice 3), and of type `/Layout` in 1.7 output. An
  overlay with text in a tagged document is **real content**: a `Span` with its own MCID, inserted in the structure
  element that held the first removed glyph, after its last surviving kid before it, with its parent-tree entry — so
  that a screen reader reads "M. [anonymisé] a déclaré", as M11 inserts annotation references into a received tree.

### Tagged structure

- A marked-content sequence whose painting is all removed loses its MCID's reference; elements left empty are removed
  bottom-up; the parent tree is rebuilt by the full rewrite (M11's rules).
- A `Figure` left with no content is removed with its `/Alt`; `/Alt`, `/ActualText`, `/E` and `/T` are scrubbed like
  any text (below). An `/ActualText` span whose glyphs are all removed goes with them; one partly removed keeps its
  text with the removed run's text replaced where it is found in it, and is dropped and reported otherwise
  (`redaction.actual-text-dropped`) — `ActualText` exists where the glyphs do not spell the text, so it cannot be
  rebuilt from them.
- `Link` and `Annot` elements of removed annotations go with them (M11).

### Beyond the page: the same text everywhere else

- **What is scrubbed**: for a hit, the matched string and the detector's canonical forms — an IBAN without spaces, a
  NIR without its groups, a telephone number in E.164 —; for a drawn mark, each run of removed glyphs as M15 extracts
  it, when it is at least `MinScrubLength` characters long (four by default).
- **Where**: every **text string** the document reaches, found by the types the Arlington model gives each key
  (ADR 44) — outline titles; annotation `/Contents`, `/T`, `/Subj` and rich text `/RC` (its XHTML text nodes); field
  `/V`, `/DV`, `/Opt`, `/TU`, `/TM`; structure `/Alt`, `/ActualText`, `/E`, `/T`; `/Info`; file specifications' `/UF`,
  `/F`, `/Desc`; page-label prefixes; optional-content group names; collection items; thread information —; **XMP**
  through M14's model, every text property, `xmpMM:History` included; **XFA** kept in step through M16 when every
  occurrence is a bound value, removed otherwise (`redaction.xfa-removed`); **name-tree keys**, renamed and the
  actions that use them rewritten, as M6 renames. A string under a key the model does not type is scanned when it
  decodes as text, scrubbed and reported (`redaction.scrubbed-untyped`).
- **How**: the occurrence replaced by the replacement text — the mark's overlay text, or "[…]" —, found with the
  folding the search used. Field appearances are regenerated after their value changes (M16), since an appearance
  shows the old value otherwise.
- **Scripts** that contain the text are removed, not edited (`redaction.script-removed`).
- **Attachments**: text-like ones — XML, JSON, text, HTML, CSV — are scanned as they stream. `Refuse`, the default,
  refuses a redaction whose text an attachment holds and names it — a Factur-X invoice's XML carries the buyer's name
  and IBAN —; `Remove` removes every attachment; `Keep` keeps them and reports each hit as a residual the caller
  accepted. An attachment that cannot be scanned is reported (`redaction.attachment-unscanned`).
- **Always**: the thumbnails of marked pages and every XMP thumbnail are removed — they picture the page before —,
  and so is the page-piece data (`/PieceInfo`) of marked pages, where an editing application may keep its own copy
  of the page.
- **Found elsewhere**: the removed text found again on other pages is reported with its page and quads
  (`redaction.also-found`), a suggestion to review; never removed without a mark.

### Fonts: glyphs that no longer draw

A subset's glyphs, and its `ToUnicode` map, tell which characters a document draws. After removal, a glyph no page
draws any more still spells what was there. With `ScrubUnusedGlyphs` (on by default):

- the fonts a removal took glyphs from are **written last**, from numbers reserved ahead, once every page, form and
  appearance using them has told which codes it still draws — one pass, as M8 finalises its subsets;
- **TrueType**: the unused glyphs' `glyf` entries emptied and `loca` rewritten, glyph identifiers kept, so no content
  stream changes; **CFF**, bare or CID-keyed: their charstrings replaced by `endchar`, subroutines kept and reported;
  **Type 3**: their `/CharProcs` emptied; `ToUnicode` entries of unused codes removed; `/Widths` and `CIDSet` kept;
- **Type 1** programs are not rewritten — their charstrings are encrypted in `eexec` — and reported
  (`redaction.font-not-scrubbed`), as are fonts not embedded, which hold no glyphs.

### Mark, review, apply

- **`Mark`** writes `/Redact` annotations: `/QuadPoints` and their union as `/Rect`, `/IC`, `/OverlayText`, `/DA`,
  `/Q`, `/Repeat`, `/RO` from the options; `/Contents` naming the kind and confidence — **never the matched text** —;
  `/NM` a deterministic identifier; `/T` the author the caller supplies; an appearance from M11's generator, which
  gains the subtype. `/Redact` is PDF 1.7: marking an older document raises its version, reported (ADR 40).
- **Others' marks** — Acrobat's, PyMuPDF's — are read by M11 and applied alike, `/RO`, `/OverlayText`, `/Repeat`
  and `/DA` honoured.
- **Review**: `PdfRedactionReview` accepts or rejects each mark by `/NM`; rejected marks are removed without being
  applied; every applied mark's annotation is removed by applying it.
- **Plan, then apply**, as M5 does: the plan says what each mark will remove and what it cannot, before anything is
  written.

### Detectors

| Detector | Finds | Checked by | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Iban` | Two letters, two check digits and a BBAN, grouped in fours or not | ISO 13616: mod 97 equal to 1, and the country's length from a table compiled from the published IBAN registry, its edition recorded | 1 when both hold; below the floor on shape alone |
| `FrenchNir` | 13 digits and a two-digit key, grouped as the carte Vitale prints them or not | Structure — sex, year, month, department with 2A and 2B, commune, order — and the key, 97 minus the number modulo 97 with 2A and 2B replaced by 19 and 18, as python-stdnum's `fr.nir` computes it | 1; a number without its key only near a cue ("n° de sécurité sociale", "NIR", "n° SS") |
| `Siren` | 9 digits, grouped 3-3-3 or not; within a SIRET; within a French VAT number `FRkk` + SIREN, whose key is (12 + 3 × (SIREN mod 97)) mod 97 | The Luhn check | High near a cue ("SIREN", "RCS"); low alone — one random 9-digit number in ten passes Luhn |
| `Siret` | 14 digits, grouped 3-3-3-5 or not | Luhn, except La Poste's SIRETs (SIREN 356 000 000), whose digits sum to a multiple of 5 instead | As SIREN |
| `Email` | An RFC 5322 address: a dot-atom local part, a domain with a dot, internationalised labels | Shape | 1 |
| `Telephone` | French numbers — 0 and nine digits in pairs, spaces, dots or hyphens —, +33 with or without the (0), and other E.164 numbers | The French plan's leading digits; lengths | High for French forms, lower for the generic one |
| `DateOfBirth` | A date, numeric or with a French or English month name, within a few words of a cue ("né le", "née le", "né(e) le", "date de naissance", "born on", "DOB") | A valid calendar date | Falling with the distance to the cue |
| `Terms` | The caller's strings — the parties' names —, folded as asked | — | 1 |
| `Regex` | The caller's pattern, `NonBacktracking` by default, M15's timeout otherwise | — | 1 |

- **Detectors run on M15's folded page text** and its offset map, so a hit maps to glyphs and quads, across line
  breaks and hyphens, through the spaces of U+00A0 and U+202F that French typesetting puts in numbers.
- **`IPdfTextDetector`** receives that text and map and returns spans with a kind and a confidence: the seam for a
  named-entity model a caller brings.
- Hits at or above `MinimumConfidence` (0.8 by default) become marks; the others are listed as candidates for review.
- Deterministic and culture-free: folding through M15's generated tables, no `CultureInfo`.

### Verification

`Apply` reopens its output — a seekable output, or a reopening factory the caller supplies; otherwise the report
says `redaction.unverified` and `VerificationPolicy.Fail` throws — and checks, under the reader's limits:

1. **The page**: M15 extracts every glyph, visible or not, whose box meets a mark: there must be none.
2. **The text**: every scrubbed string and its canonical forms, searched through the whole document — pages,
   annotations, fields, outline, metadata, structure, file names —: no hit.
3. **The bytes**: every string and every decoded stream scanned for each scrubbed string of four characters or more
   in UTF-8, UTF-16BE, UTF-16LE, PDFDocEncoding and Windows-1252, streamed with a window the length of the longest
   encoding: no hit, but in an attachment the caller chose to keep.
4. **The pixels**: each redacted image decoded again, its samples under each mark uniform.
5. **The file**: one revision (M4), no byte after `%%EOF`, no `/Redact` annotation left.

A failure throws `PdfRedactionVerificationException` with the report; the output written must not be used, and the
documentation says so where it shows `Apply`.

### Inspection: the disclosure profile

`ValidationProfile.Disclosure` — `disclosure`, version 1 — is a second built-in profile of M2's engine (ADR 36),
holding two families. The structural profile does not change, so no manifest `findings` move; the manifest gains
`expect.disclosureFindings`, written from walks independent of our reader — pikepdf, `pdfinfo -js`, ExifTool, and
Didier Stevens' `pdfid` over qpdf's uncompressed rewrite.

The rules judge facts others gathered: M15's inventory and glyph visibility, M4's revision history, M11's
visibility evaluator, M16's form model. M19 adds one analysis, **concealment**: a device on M15's interpreter that
flags a glyph wholly under an opaque rectangle or image painted after it (`CoveredByPath`, beside M15's
`CoveredByImage`), a glyph whose fill colour lies within a ΔE threshold of the opaque fill beneath it or of the
page's white (`LowContrast`, judged only in device grey, RGB and CMYK), and a glyph drawn smaller than a threshold
(`Tiny`); the thresholds fixed in slice 8 and recorded.

| Rule | Severity | Checks |
|---|---|---|
| `action.javascript` | Warning | A script anywhere: the document name tree, an open or additional action, a link, an outline item, a field's format, keystroke, validate or calculate script, an XFA script |
| `action.automatic` | Information | An action run without the user: an `/OpenAction` other than a destination; catalogue `/AA` (close, save, print); page `/AA` (open, close); annotation `/AA` on page open, close, visible, invisible |
| `action.launch` | Warning | A Launch action — a program run or a file opened |

### Bounds (invariant 12, [ADR 34](../adr/0034-every-valid-pdf-is-readable-and-the-readers-guards-are.md))

| Bound | Kind | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Marks per page | None: indexed in a grid over the page box, a glyph tested against the cells it touches | A file may carry thousands of `/Redact` annotations; the cost stays linear |
| Curve subdivision where a stroke crosses a mark: depth 24 | Internal constant | A cubic crosses a rectangle's edges at most twelve times, so the pieces stay few; past depth 24 a piece is under a millionth of its curve, and is removed |
| Segments of a flattened curve | Internal constant, recorded in slice 2 | Only fidelity near the mark is at stake: the subtraction is exact on the flattened polygon |
| Operations, glyphs and state depth on a page | M15's guards | The pipeline runs the interpreter |
| Samples of an image redacted | Checked arithmetic on width × components × bits, under `MaxDecodedStreamLength`; rows streamed | Dimensions come from the file |
| Attachments and streams scanned during verification | Streamed under `MaxDecodedStreamLength`; a stream cut at a limit makes verification fail, never pass | A limit is the reader's, not the file's |
| Glyph usage per font | A bit set over the font's code space, at most 65,536 codes | Format limit |
| A regular expression's match time | M15's `MatchTimeout` when backtracking | The caller's pattern on hostile text |

### The command-line tool

```
redact    IN -o OUT ( --mark PAGE:X0,Y0,X1,Y1 … | --search TEXT … | --terms FILE
                    | --detect iban,nir,siren,siret,email,phone,dob | --regex PATTERN | --apply-marks )
          [--review OUT-review.pdf] [--overlay-text T] [--colour C] [--min-confidence N]
          [--images refuse|remove] [--attachments refuse|remove|keep] [--allow-invalidating-signatures]
sanitise  IN [-o OUT] [--inspect] [--profile active-content|hidden-data|all] [--categories …] [--keep …]
```

`--review` writes the marked copy and stops; `--apply-marks` applies the marks a file already carries, ours or
Acrobat's. `sanitise --inspect` prints the disclosure report and writes nothing. Both take `--json` and M6's exit
codes; a refusal by policy is exit code 4, a failed verification exit code 1.

## Traps

- **A box is not a redaction.** Text under a rectangle is still text: selectable, searchable, extractable. Only
  removal counts, and `hidden.text-covered` reports the documents that got this wrong before they reached us.
- **An incremental update is an archive of what it replaced.** Every revision is still in the file; a redaction
  written that way publishes the original. Full rewrite, every time.
- **The OCR layer is not the scan.** Removing the words of an OCR layer leaves the pixels that show them; a mark over
  an image the core cannot decode is refused, not covered.
- **The same text lives in many places**: a bookmark, the title, the XMP history, a figure's alternative text,
  `ActualText`, a field's value and its appearance, a note's popup, a named destination, an attachment, a thumbnail of
  the page as it was. Scrub by the model's types, then verify by scanning bytes, because the list is never complete.
- **Page-piece data can be a second copy of the document.** Illustrator keeps its editable artwork — and a designer's
  local paths — in `AIPDFPrivateData` streams under `/PieceInfo`; the IRS publication in the corpus carries it on both
  pages. Redacting the page and leaving that is redacting nothing: page-piece data of a marked page always goes.
- **A subset spells what it drew.** The glyphs a document no longer draws, and their `ToUnicode` entries, still say
  which characters it held.
- **Positions leak.** A removed name's width, and the kerning around it, narrow the candidates; the overlay shows as
  much. Reflowing is not the answer for a received document — documenting the limit is.
- **Hidden content moves the pen.** Removing a glyph must keep its advance; removing a painted path must keep the clip
  it set (M11's trap).
- **Word spacing applies to one byte.** `Tw` adds to the single-byte code 32 only — never to a two-byte code, even one
  that draws a space (ISO 32000-2 §9.3.3); a displacement that assumes otherwise moves the rest of the line.
- **Inline image data can contain `EI`.** A redacted inline image is written as an XObject.
- **Masks are pictures of the shape.** An image's soft mask or stencil under a mark shows the outline of what was
  there.
- **A detector is a suggestion.** One random 9-digit number in ten passes Luhn; a date near "né le" may be a
  signature date. Marks are reviewed before they apply, and the confidence floor says which to review first.
- **La Poste's SIRETs fail Luhn**, and Corsica's NIRs have letters where the key's arithmetic expects digits.
- **French numbers are grouped with narrow no-break spaces** (U+202F): a detector that splits on ordinary spaces misses
  most of them.
- **An IBAN in XML has no spaces.** Scrub and verify the canonical form, not only the printed one.
- **Removing scripts can break a form**: its calculations stop, its formats stop reformatting. Recognised Acrobat
  formats can be kept; the report says what else stops.
- **A dynamic XFA form without its XFA is a page saying "please wait"** — reported, never presented as sanitised
  content.
- **Removing metadata can break a claim**: PDF/UA wants the title, PDF/A the claims themselves. `Metadata` keeps them.
- **Sanitising a signed document is re-making it.** No signature survives a full rewrite; the flattened appearance
  shows what it looked like and proves nothing.
